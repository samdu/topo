# Timing a launch and a turn

Where the time goes is read off marks the app writes itself. `Perf.mark(_:)` (`Packages/TopoCore/Sources/TopoCore/Perf.swift`) logs one `notice` line per named moment under subsystem `zone.hexagon.topo`, category `perf`: `mark t=<epoch ms> <name> [detail]`. A mark carries a name and nothing of the person's: no words, no tokens, a request's method, route, byte count and number at most, and a turn's `turn=<id>`, the first eight letters or digits of its random nonce (`Harness.turnMark`). The route is the path with every segment that is not one of `Forwarder.routeSegments` replaced by `*`, and no query (`Forwarder.routeForMark`). `turn.reply.shown` is the reply handed to the view, a frame before it is drawn; `resident.spawned` is a launch that succeeded (its `cpu`, and `resident.ready`'s, is the CPU the app's process has used so far, in milliseconds, so the difference between the two is what the whole app spent while Claude Code started, the guest included), and one that failed is `resident.launch.failed`.

## From inside

`topo perf` (`Apps/Topo/Tools/PerfTool.swift`, `docs/guest.md`) reads these marks back off the whole of this launch's unified log through `topo log`'s reader and says a turn as what happened, in order, in a person's words (`TurnTimeline`). A turn is found by its `turn=<id>`: it begins at its first `turn.line` (its first `turn.attempt` if the line was another launch's), or for a turn with a `turn.spoken` at the `voice.released` whose `voice.heard` came last before that line by the marks' order, and it is finished when each of its `turn.attempt`s has its `turn.attempt.end` and the last did not end `unsaved` (the guest still answering while iCloud is behind); a turn in flight, the one asking among them, is none, and one the lease said another device answers ended with no reply here. Between its start and its end, the marks of that time are its events, sorted by time, each titled by what happened and timed as the wait that ended with it, so the times sum to the turn's: the hearing, each attempt, the person's words saved (`turn.lease.fresh`, `turn.saved` or else `turn.person.saved`; `turn.unsaved`; where the guest had the words first, heard ahead, the save ran while it worked and is said beside the turn from the first attempt, outside the sum), Claude Code having them (the first `turn.guest.write`) and starting on them (the `turn.guest.init` after it, so a process still launching, resuming or booting spends its start there), and for each of the turn's own model requests a tool's run where one came since the request before it ended (its last `turn.guest.toolResult` before the request), the request's going and the model's wait together (to its `turn.guest.message own`, with what its upload read: the upload mark of its `guest` and `request`, wherever it fell), and its stream (the first `turn.guest.message.done own` after it in the marks' order, before the next request's start); a sub-agent's requests run beside the turn's and are none of its stages, and a retry, which names no request, is said beside the turn where it came and is none of them either, so a request the turn's own retried has its wait in the stage before its answer. What the stream cannot give is apart from it: the upload apart from the model's wait, the answer's head apart from its first byte, and what a request is made of, which only reading the body would give; then the first words drawn, the guest's result, the reply saved or failed, and the reply drawn (its own `turn.reply.shown`, where an answered turn stops: the pass's work after it is not the person's wait) or, for an attempt that ended otherwise, the end. Where another turn was under way inside this one's time (one that has not ended counts to this turn's end if it began inside it, and else to its own last mark), the untagged marks of the shared stretch belong to either, so the stretch is one event, `mixed`, from its start to its end: nothing inside it is said, this turn's own attempts and hearing there included (the attempts are still counted in `sends=`), so nothing takes part of its time; the wait up to where the stretch began is this turn's own, `wait` ("Another message began"), and stretches that do not touch are apart, with this turn's events between them read. The voice's first sound (its own `speak.begin turn=<id>` to the first `speak.firstFrame` after it and before another `speak.begin`, by the marks' order, since two can share a millisecond) is beside the events too. The whole launch is read, so a turn is never cut at its start; a launch so long that the read runs past the tool service's bound is answered as that bound's timeout (exit 4), never as no turns. What it answers is titles, times and counts, and each turn's `record:` line, which sums the events by kind for the mind to keep in its memory across launches; the app keeps none.

## A run with nobody at the phone

`scripts/perf-run.sh --device <id> [--build | --app <Topo.app>] "question" ["question" …]` installs a build, launches it cold with `TOPO_PERF_SEND` in its environment, and copies the marks off when the run is done. `PerfRun` (`Apps/Topo/PerfRun.swift`) is what reads the variable: it starts `tmp/topo-perf.log` in the app's container empty, has every mark written there as well as to the log, sends the first question as soon as the scene is up and each next `TOPO_PERF_GAP` seconds (5) after the reply to the last, through the harness's own line (`willSend`, `retry`) as the composer does, and marks `perf.run.done answered=<n>/<m>`. Each `perf.question <i>/<m>` carries `thermal=<0–3>` (`ProcessInfo.thermalState`: nominal, fair, serious, critical), since a phone warmed by earlier runs interprets the guest slower and a run is only comparable with one at the same state. A question counts as answered when its reply is in the transcript, which is waited for up to a minute after its turn ends (a turn saved as a limb's is answered by another device); the run ends at the first question that gets none, and, after the log has been read and any words the launch before left on the line have been sent (`perf.line.waiting`), sends nothing if words are still waiting. `TOPO_PERF_SEND` is a JSON array of strings, which the script makes from its arguments. One launch is one run, however many windows it restores. The script polls the file with `devicectl device copy from` until that mark is in it, taking only a file whose first mark is no older than its own launch (a copy can reach the phone before the new process has emptied the last run's file), and exits 4 when fewer questions were answered than asked. A blank question is refused before anything is installed (exit 2).

With `--spoken` (`TOPO_PERF_SPOKEN=1`) the questions are sent as spoken turns once the voice is resident, their replies are read aloud from the phone, and the next question waits for the reading to end. The run waits up to two minutes for the voice, since a fresh install compiles its model on the first load (`voice.load.begin` to `voice.load.end`, 35–44 s on an iPhone 15 Pro), and marks `perf.voice.unready` if it sends without it.

With `--fresh` (`TOPO_PERF_SETUP=1`) the run is a first run: the script deletes the app from the phone, which takes its container and every download and compiled model with it, then installs (so `--app` or `--build` is required, exit 2 without), and the app waits for every setup line to be done before its first question, up to 50 minutes, marking `perf.setup.done`, `perf.setup.failed <part>` as soon as a line fails (nothing retries it without a foreground), or `perf.setup.unfinished`. A reinstalled phone is the same device to the lease, its name kept in the keychain, and it is signed out. Where the lease names it, it opens as the primary and whoever is at the phone signs in. Where the lease names another holder, a hub included, fresh or lapsed, it opens as a viewer: whoever is at the phone makes it the primary ("Make this device the primary…"), which goes through only once the other holder has stopped heartbeating, then signs in. The phone stays unlocked with Topo in front throughout, or the suspended time is inside the timings. The script deletes nothing unless `--app` (or `--build`'s product) is a bundle whose identifier is Topo's, asks the phone whether Topo is installed rather than reading an error's words, takes only a well-formed answer, and stops when there is none or the delete fails, since a container left behind would make a warm run read as a first one. The script's timeout is an hour unless `--timeout` says. The first-run account is then read off the marks: from `app.init.begin` to each entry's `download.present`, `guest.import.end`, `ear.load.end` and `voice.load.end`.

So one run times a cold launch, a question asked into a cold app, and as many warm turns as there are further questions. The questions are sent as the person and land in their transcript.

`PerfRun` is in a release build because a release build is what is timed. The variable reaches the process only from a launch by `devicectl` or Xcode, from a Mac the phone trusts; an ordinary launch has none.

The phone has to be unlocked: `devicectl` refuses to launch an app on a locked one, and the script exits 3 saying so.

## Without the script

The same marks are in the phone's unified log while `perf` is not switched `off` (`topo log level`, `docs/guest.md`; the marks file and `Perf.observer` get every mark whatever the level): `sudo log collect --device-udid <hardware udid> --last 15m --output run.logarchive`, then `log show run.logarchive --predicate 'subsystem == "zone.hexagon.topo" AND category == "perf"' --style compact`. The collect needs the phone awake and unlocked and no `idevicesyslog` attached to it; from buddybox it works over the Wi-Fi pairing (`sudo` is needed, with the login password), and `--last 24h` reaches as far back as the phone keeps, a few hours of a busy day. The archive is the phone's whole log, which the predicate narrows at `log show`; the collect changes nothing on the phone, so it is the read to make while somebody is using it. A file the app writes into its own container, copied out with `xcrun devicectl device copy from --domain-type appDataContainer` as `perf-run.sh` does with the marks file, is the other read.

## What the process costs

`EnergyPulse` (`Apps/Topo/EnergyPulse.swift`) writes one `energy` mark a minute for as long as the process runs: this process's CPU as a share of one core and in seconds, its idle and interrupt wakeups a second, both from the kernel's `TASK_POWER_INFO` counters, its memory footprint now and its peak since launch in MiB (`TASK_VM_INFO`'s `phys_footprint`, the figure jetsam judges, and `ledger_phys_footprint_peak`; `footprint unread` when the kernel refuses), the battery level and charger, the thermal state, Low Power Mode, whether the app is in front, and what it was doing — the guest resident or not, a turn in flight, the microphone open, a reply being spoken, a background hold standing, read as the minute ends, so a minute a turn began and ended inside says no turn. The process's first pulse ends `first since launch`: its CPU runs from `EnergyPulse.start()`, near the end of the app's init, so what the process spent before that is in no pulse, and the peak is the whole process's. On a launch in front, signed in as the primary with the guest's files on the phone, the guest's boot and Claude Code's start normally begin in that first minute and can run into the next; a launch in the background, signed out or still downloading may boot the guest in a later minute, which no mark names (it boots in the first one when it comes forward, signs in or finishes before that pulse), and a viewer runs no guest. A second mark, `energy threads`, names the threads the minute's CPU went to, the busiest first, so a minute that cost something says where: each thread's seconds are summed under its name before a group under 0.05 s is left out, groups past the busiest eight are `other`, and what threads that ended inside the minute spent, which the kernel no longer names, is `unattributed`. A minute whose threads could not be listed says `not read this interval`, and the next listing is a baseline rather than a breakdown, so no thread's lifetime is ever counted as a minute's. Both marks read the same interval, timed by the continuous clock, so a change to the phone's clock does not move a rate. Both are marks like any other (unified log, `perf` category, and a timed run's file), and both are appended to `tmp/topo-energy.log` in the app's container, started afresh past 512 KB, which `devicectl device copy from --device <id> --domain-type appDataContainer --domain-identifier zone.hexagon.topo --source tmp/topo-energy.log --destination <file>` reads off a phone nobody is at. A guest process's thread is counted as `guest`, never by the command it was named after. The diagnostics screen shows the last pulse.

MetricKit's daily payload (`MXMetricPayload`: this process's CPU time, display luminance, bytes over cellular and Wi-Fi, time in the foreground, the background and background audio) lands through the same subscriber, up to a day after it was gathered, as a one-line `energy metrickit` mark and a JSON file under Application Support/metrickit (not backed up), the newest fourteen of each kind kept, metrics and diagnostics; the diagnostics screen counts them. A TestFlight build's payloads also reach Xcode's Organizer under Energy, which a dev-signed build's never do.

Two launch arguments are for a profiling run from a Mac and nothing else: `--stay-awake` holds auto-lock off for the run as a standing `AudioSession` screen claim, beside the microphone's and the speaker's (a locked phone takes no launch and no copy), and `--still-mascot` draws Topo once, as Reduce Motion does, so a state can be measured with and without him moving. A run is the phone unlocked on a charger, one state at a time for four or five minutes (the first pulse after a launch carries the launch itself), and the lines compared by their `cpu` and `wakeups` fields.
