# The log, the lease and the packages

The records every bundle shares and the Swift packages that hold them. The rule over all of it, *CloudKit is truth, sockets are speed*, is in CLAUDE.md; the design behind the log and the memory is `docs/design.md`.

## The records

The transcript is an append-only log: one record per turn, per-device sequence numbers, never overwrite. The role record (`role/<device>`, `DeviceRole`) is how one device changes another's role, written by the taker in the make-primary control; a device reads its own at launch and on each answering pass. The primary lease is the one mutable record: atomic claim, 10s expiry judged on `mach_continuous_time` (`PrimaryLease.continuousUptime`) as well as the wall clock — not `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, which on Darwin is time-daemon-disciplined and can follow a backward system-time step, the case the fence exists for — 5s heartbeat the holder runs itself, probe-driven handover, a displaced holder yields rather than reclaims, and only the hub takes the lease from a live holder. A turn written as primary rides in one atomic batch with a heartbeat of the lease (`TurnWriter.append(…, renewing:)`), so a displaced device writes nothing; the phone's reply uses it and the hub's turns will. A holder whose heartbeats ran late is not displaced: its lease lapsed locally with the record still its own, so the batch reads the record and, while it is still that lease (same holder, same epoch), carries a fresh claim over the version read, one epoch on, in place of the heartbeat. A claim by any other device, before that read or between it and the save, refuses the batch as it refuses a heartbeat, and nothing but a write of the lease that lands makes `isPrimary()` true again after the lapse. Within one `PrimaryLease` the lease's reads and writes go one at a time, in the order they were asked for (`PrimaryLease`'s gate), so a reply's batch asked for while a heartbeat is out waits for that heartbeat's answer and goes over the version it wrote; `abandon()` alone does not wait, and an answer from before it holds nothing. So that a stalled request does not hold the calls behind it without limit, the lease's saves and fetches are asked of the database bounded (`answering(within:)`): over CloudKit each is given `LeaseTiming.patience`, 4 s, as its request timeout, and one that runs out is `unavailable`, which may still have been applied; a renewal that landed is found later as the record at the epoch held, at a version the lease has not heard of, and is taken as its own, since no claim leaves the epoch where it was. A claim that landed is not: the record names a device and an endpoint, not an instance, so this device's lease at a later epoch this instance has sent is that claim or the claim of another instance of the device (a harness dropped and made again), and the lease takes neither as held: it stops counting itself primary, yields to nobody, refuses a batch, and the next `acquire()` or `takeOver()` claims over the record, one epoch on, as over any lease not yielded to. The bound is a request's, not a call's: a pass is a read and a write, so one can hold the next heartbeat up to 8 s, a call whose write loses to another writer goes again (three passes at most), and a heartbeat sent a full interval after the last lands inside the lease only behind a single request that ran out, with a second to spare. Past that the lease lapses locally and the next turn or batch claims it afresh. A link on which requests take longer than the bound loses every one of them, where an unbounded request would be answered late. A `Surface` record (`surface-<slot>`) is a phone widget slot's document for the watch, written only by a compare-and-set on the change tag of the read it was made from, in which the later revision of the same `runner` wins and another runner's record is replaced; it is never deleted, since a delete carries no tag, but cleared with a tombstone (`cleared`, no document, no images) when the slot is cleared, the phone that saved it signs out, or the primary sweeps a record another runner saved (`docs/widgets.md`, *The record*); its `updated` field needs to be marked queryable in the development schema for the watch's subscription.

A turn's round trips before its person's turn is in the log, on a device whose lease is fresh by its own clocks and whose brain owes the log nothing, are the log's change feed, its fetch by ID of each device's next sequence number, and one save: the person's turn in an atomic batch with the lease's renewal (`PrimaryLease.heartbeat(saving:)`). On a device that last held the lease the brain has the words before any of them (`docs/harness.md`, *A turn ahead of iCloud*), and on any other after the lease's call alone. The renewal is a compare-and-set on the version this device last wrote, the same one `acquire()` makes after its fetch, so a claim by another device since the last heartbeat refuses the batch with nothing written and the turn goes the long way. The long way, which is also every turn on a lease lapsed or never held and every turn with an owed reply to settle, adds the lease's fetch and write: `TurnRunner.run` reads the log while it takes the lease, since the read asks nothing of the lease and is used only by its holder. A retry that finds the person's turn already in the log saved nothing, so it heartbeats the lease alone before answering. The writer does not fetch its own next sequence a second time for a transcript read for that append (`Transcript.free`, `justRead:`): a record landing there afterwards is found by the create-only save, and an append continuing from a transcript (`continuing:`) has to take the sequence number the transcript was checked against: when that number has gone, to another writer's turn or to an append of the same writer made meanwhile, it throws `incompleteTranscript` with nothing written, since the transcript's heads are then not the log's. On the phone the words stay on the line, and its retry reads again and appends under the same nonce.

## The packages and their tests

Core logic ships as Swift packages whose tests run against an in-memory database behind the package's own CloudKit protocol; no test needs a signed-in device. `Packages/TopoCore` is the log, the lease, the device directory and the memory (`MemoryStore`, one create-only record per revision; `MemoryVault`, which reads those revisions as files, with the newer head of a forked path the file and every other head a conflict copy under Obsidian Sync's name; and `VaultMirror`, the actor that keeps a directory on disk in step with them both ways): `RecordDatabase` is the protocol over the CloudKit calls used (compare-and-set save, fetch, query, and the whole of a type from the zone's change feed, which is how the log and the device directory are read: a match-all query needs a queryable index on the record name that the development schema never builds), `ZoneDatabase` adds a delete (a record already gone is not an error) and a type's changes from a change token, deletions included, with an expired token answered `tokenExpired` so a reader starts again from the beginning; a field may hold assets (`FieldValue.assets`, a `CKAsset` per file). `CloudKitRecordDatabase` is the adapter to both, and `TopoCoreTesting` holds the in-memory database with CloudKit's conflict semantics.

A read of a whole type (`RecordDatabase.records(ofType:)`) is the zone's change feed, and over CloudKit it is answered from `ZoneMirror` (`Packages/TopoCore/Sources/TopoCore/ZoneMirror.swift`): the process's copy of the zone, every type, with the feed's token after the last page it took. The first read of a launch walks the feed from the beginning; every read after, by whichever object over that zone, asks only from the token and answers from the copy, which is what a walk from the beginning would return at that moment. Reads go one at a time and each asks the feed, so one begun after a save returns it. An expired token is a walk from the beginning. The copy is also kept on disk between launches, so a launch asks the feed from where the last one stopped: one binary plist per zone under the caches directory (`zone-mirror/`), holding the records, the token and the iCloud account it was read as (`FileManager.ubiquityIdentityToken`), written whole after a read that changed anything and protected until first unlock. A file that is missing, unreadable or of another account is a walk from the beginning, as is a device with no iCloud account to name, which keeps nothing. Sign-out removes the copies and drops the process's mirrors, so the next read makes one under the iCloud account there is then (`CloudKitRecordDatabase.forgetMirrors`, from `Harness.forget`); a read asked for before that, under way or waiting behind another, throws `CancellationError` and pulls, holds and keeps nothing after it. `ZoneMirrorTests` holds all of it against a feed in memory.

A container nothing has written to answers two more ways, both absorbed by the adapter and both invisible until a fresh account takes its first turn: a query on a record type the development schema has never seen fails `unknownItem` rather than returning nothing (answered `[]`), and an empty string list on a first save makes the server refuse the whole atomic batch with "Syntax error in request" (left off the wire, since a list with no elements has no type the schema can be grown from).

`Packages/TopoLink` is the socket layer: the lease probe over TCP (one line each way), the listener that answers it and advertises the device as `_topo._tcp`, and the browse that says who is on the LAN; its tests run on loopback.

`swift test` from a package directory runs its suite. `TopoTests` drives the phone's `Harness` itself over the in-memory log, with the guest behind a scripted `GuestConversation` (the unsettled line on disk and its relaunch, a failed model call, not-primary and displaced turns, the role-record handover, the answering loop). No automated suite reaches the CloudKit service: `CloudKitMappingTests` covers the adapter's record and error mapping offline, so the account, the schema, partial failures and the zone change feed are exercised only by Womble's manual self-test (`CloudKitSelfTest`) on a signed-in device.
