# The mind on the hub

With the switch on ("Run the mind on this Mac", off by default, kept in the hub's defaults as `mind.on`, which the menu and a shell both set: *From a shell* below), the hub holds the primary lease while the Mac is awake and answers the person's turns from the CloudKit log through a resident native Claude Code (`NativeBrain`). The phone writes its turn to the log as a limb, defers to the hub's fresh lease (`HubLeaseProbe`, `docs/harness.md`) and draws the reply. Off, the hub claims nothing and runs no mind; the pairing code, the device list and the LAN surfaces carry on either way. The loop is `HubMind` (`Packages/TopoMind/Sources/TopoMind/HubMind.swift`), which `HubModel` (`Apps/TopoHub/HubModel.swift`) makes once the probe listener's endpoint is known and drives from the switch, the workspace's sleep and wake, the push and quit.

## The lease

The hub takes the lease from whoever holds it (`PrimaryLease.takeOver()`) once each time the mind starts: the switch turned on, the hub launched with it on, each wake from sleep, and Try again after a stop. Before it, the start holds off while a phone may be answering (`HubMind.heldOff`): while the lease is a fresh lease of a device that is not a hub, and a person's turn at the log's head is unanswered and was said less than `HubMind.standardHoldBound` (10 min) ago, the hub claims nothing and runs no pass, reading the lease (one fetch by ID) and the log every `standardHoldCheck` (2 s), and the menu says the phone is answering. The takeover goes when the head is answered or gone on from (the phone's reply landed), when the lease lapses or names another holder (a phone that went stops renewing, so it is taken over one lease duration and at most one check after its last renewal), or when the turn, or the hold itself, reaches the bound; a lease or log that cannot be read holds, within the same bound. A takeover over a phone's lease with nothing of the person's waiting goes at once. The bound is what a phone that is alive and never answers the turn costs it: the phone's guest bounds a turn's silence (3 min, after which the turn fails and the phone's own loop asks again) and not its length, so a phone renewing its lease is taken to be answering. The loop has not begun while the start holds off, so no pass's `acquire()` claims over the phone meanwhile. A takeover that cannot be sent is sent again, backing off to 30 s, until it is answered. From then on the lease is kept by its own heartbeats, which every answered write starts (5 s, a 10 s duration), and by the runner's writes, each a batch with a heartbeat. Before every pass a lease this Mac does not hold — lapsed after late heartbeats, or taken by a phone — is asked for with `acquire()`, never `takeOver()`. A phone that claimed over the hub's live lease is a holder the hub yielded to at its next heartbeat or batch, and keeps the lease until it lapses: it is not claimed back on a timer, and the two do not flip. A phone's lease the hub has not yielded to is another matter: the phone answers no probe (its lease names no endpoint, so `SocketLeaseProbe` says no), so `acquire()` claims over it on that one probe, as it would over any holder it cannot reach. That is the hub whose own lease lapsed (late heartbeats, a network gap) and which a phone claimed meanwhile: the hub takes it back at its next pass, and the phone yields at its next heartbeat. Every stop is `PrimaryLease.abandon()`, which stops the heartbeats at once and makes any write sent after it hold nothing, and then `PrimaryLease.settled()`, which returns once a write of the lease already out at the abandon has been answered: a heartbeat's, or the renewal a pass sent beside its read of the log, which the pass itself waits for on every way out (`TurnRunner.answerPending`). The database gives each request the lease's patience (4 s), and `settled()` gives up a second after that whatever the database does. A request that ran out, or one a store ended at once because the stop cancelled the task that sent it (a heartbeat's, a reply's batch; the pass's renewal is in a task of its own and is always waited for), may still be applied by the server, so a write of the lease, or a reply's batch with its heartbeat (`heartbeat(saving:)`, at the same gate and under the same patience), can land after a stop has returned; it was built before the abandon, so the record it leaves expires within one duration (10 s) of the abandon, as every write the session sent does. The record lapses at most one duration after the abandon, and the phone claims it at its next turn or pass. A stop writes no release: it would be a write sent after the abandon, which is what the lease refuses, over a version that a renewal landed unanswered may have moved. Every claim a session of the mind makes — the start's takeover, a pass's `acquire()`, and the runner's within the pass (`TurnRunner.answerPending(since:choosing:)`) — is made since the mark of the lease's abandons the session took as it began (`PrimaryLease.abandons`, `acquire(since:)`, `takeOver(since:)`), and the lease refuses one made since a later abandon, with nothing read or written. A stop moves the mind's generation before it abandons, so a session whose mark was read after the abandon is already over; one read before it is refused by the lease however late its claim arrives, so a stop that lands while a pass reads the log, renews the lease or asks for a lapsed one leaves nothing claimed and the brain unasked, whatever the order of the actors' jobs.

## Starting and stopping

A start goes in this order, so the lease is never held by a mind that cannot answer: the mind's home is made (`MindHome.prepare`), Claude Code is asked whether it is signed in (`MindLogin.check`), the resident process is started, the runner is made (its writer is this Mac's, read from the log), then the lease is taken over and the first pass runs.

- **Switch off**: the pass under way is cancelled, the lease abandoned, the session waited for, any write of the lease already out waited for (`settled()`), and the process shut down and reaped before the call returns. The runner checks cancellation before it asks the brain and before each append, the abandoned lease refuses a batch, and the session's claims are refused since the abandon (*The lease*), so nothing is claimed and no turn sent after the switch; a write of the lease, or a reply's batch, sent before the switch and run out of its patience or cancelled with it may still land (*The lease*).
- **Will-sleep** (`NSWorkspace.willSleepNotification`): the same, but the process is left; the Mac suspends it with everything else. The notification's handler waits up to 2 s for the stop, so the lease is abandoned before the system goes on to sleep; a write of the lease still out after those 2 s (the stop waits up to 5 s for one) may be answered as the Mac sleeps or at its wake, and expires within 10 s of the abandon. A turn the sleep cut off is listened to again on the same process at wake rather than asked twice. Nothing runs while asleep, a dark wake's timers included, because the loop is gone.
- **Did-wake** (`NSWorkspace.didWakeNotification`): a start, with its one takeover.
- **Quit** (`applicationShouldTerminate`): as switch off, with the switch kept.
- **Signed out, or a mind that cannot run**: a login that says signed out, a turn that fails `NativeBrainError.notSignedIn`, a process that cannot be started, a ledger that cannot be read, or a home that cannot be made stops the mind as switch off does and leaves it stopped, saying why, until Try again, the switch, or a wake starts it again. The phone answers once the lease lapses. A turn that ends `unresolved` or `failed` is a failed pass, not a stop; an `unresolved` turn waits for the person, as on the phone.
- **A stalled turn** (`NativeBrainError.stalled`, *A hung mind* below) stops the mind in the same way, and the menu, the status and the `hub.mind.stopped stalled silent|tooLong` mark say so. It is not started again on a timer, which would take the lease from the phone only to stall again.
- **`kill -TERM <pid>`**: the hub handles SIGTERM as Quit, so the lease is abandoned at once (no renewal after the signal), the mind's process is torn down and reaped, and the hub exits; if that takes longer than 10 s it exits anyway. The record lapses 10 s after its last renewal, and the phone claims it at its next turn or pass.
- **`kill -9 <pid>`**, or a crash: nothing of the hub runs, so renewals stop with it and the record lapses as above. The mind's process has a watcher beside it (`MindProcess`, a `/bin/sh` in a group of its own reading a pipe only the hub writes), which the hub's end wakes, and which kills the mind's group and every descendant of it at once. Claude Code with its stdin closed carries the turn under way to its end, tools included, before it exits (2.1.325), so without the watcher an orphan would. It holds no lease, can write no record and has nowhere to send a reply. The next start also reaps any resident the last one left (`resident.json` in the config directory: the pid and its start time, so a later process given the same pid is left alone) before it starts its own, since both would write the one session.

## A hung mind

A turn has two bounds, both from when the hub begins listening for it (`NativeBrain.Configuration`): no line at all on Claude Code's stdout for `silenceBound` (3 min, `NativeBrain.standardSilence`), or no result within `turnBound` (30 min, `standardTurn`). Every line counts, whatever it is. The process runs with `--include-partial-messages`, so Claude Code 2.1.325 writes a line for each piece of a reply or of a tool call's input as the model makes it, `system/thinking_tokens` while it thinks, `system/status` while a request waits, and a `tool_progress` heartbeat every 30 s while a tool runs; its longest quiet in a working turn is the wait for a request's first token. Silence is a process that has stopped: wedged, or its event loop stuck. A tool that never returns heartbeats all the while (Bash ends a command at its timeout), and the turn bound is what ends it.

A turn past either bound is given up on: the brain marks its process never to be asked again, the pass ends, the mind halts (the lease abandoned first, then the process torn down), and it stays stopped as above. The turn's record in the ledger says what comes of it: one Claude Code had taken up (its input echoed) is `unresolved`, as one cut off by a process's exit is, since it may have run tools; one never echoed is cleared and asked again by the next request. Nothing of it can be written by the hub: the pass wrote nothing, the abandoned lease refuses a batch, and no reply was finished, so none is owed.

The phone answers it. Its `answerPending` skips only the turns its own brain holds as unresolved, so once the hub's lease lapses (10 s after the halt) it answers the turn at its next turn or pass, under the reply nonce both derive from the log's heads (`TurnRunner.replyNonce(for:)`), whose marker makes a second write of that reply impossible on any device. When the hub starts again after that, the log has moved past the turn and the hub counts it told. A hub started again (Try again, a wake, the switch) before the phone answered holds the lease once more, and the turn waits for the person to ask again, as any cut-off turn does.

## From a shell

The person is usually away from the Mac, and the session driving the hub has a shell and no menu. `scripts/hub-mind.sh on|off|status` drives the switch: `on` and `off` write `mind.on` in the hub's defaults (domain `zone.hexagon.topo.hub`, as `defaults write zone.hexagon.topo.hub mind.on -bool true` would) and send the running hub SIGUSR1, then wait for its status to show the switch applied and print it. The hub follows the defaults (`MindSwitch.follow`) when the mind is made at launch, so a hub launched with the switch on starts the mind; at its 10 s refresh; and at SIGUSR1, which only ever means "follow the defaults now", never a flip. A stopped mind (signed out, stalled) is started again from a shell by `off` then `on`, as Try again does; `on` over a switch already on changes nothing. `status` prints what the menu's mind section says, from `hub-status.json` in the mind's root (`HubStatus`), which the hub writes at every change and refresh: whether the hub is running and its pid, the switch, the login, who holds the lease, the mind's phase by kind, the mind's own line, the last pass, and whether the nudge's channel's key is saved (with the last save's failure by kind when it is not) and how many handshakes on the channel failed since launch. A status whose pid is not a running TopoHub is said to be a stopped hub's, and that pid is never signalled. Nothing in the status is a word of a turn or a reason Claude Code gave: its mind line is `NativeBrain.describe(reasons: false)`, which says a start failed and not why; the menu shows why. The script reaches an unsandboxed hub, the one `scripts/hub-dev-build.sh` builds; a sandboxed build keeps its defaults in its container. Killing the hub by the status's pid is the rollback: `kill -TERM` quits it cleanly, and `kill -9` leaves nothing that answers or holds the lease (*Starting and stopping*).

## Triggers

A pass is `TurnRunner.answerPending`. One runs at a time: on a trigger, and on a poll as the backstop, every 5 s while this Mac holds the lease (`HubMind.standardAnsweringPoll`) and every 10 s while it does not (`standardPoll`). A trigger while the loop waits starts the pass at once; any number of triggers before it begins are that one pass, and any number during a pass are one more pass after it, since the pass may have read the log before their turn landed. A pass with nothing waiting is one request, the zone's change feed from its token, and touches neither the lease nor the brain: the fetch by ID of each device's next sequence number that completes a read of the log (`TurnLog.probed`) only ever finds turns the feed is behind on, which leave the read incomplete and the pass with nothing to do, so a pass makes it only when the feed shows a person's turn to answer or a reply the brain owes. That is half the read the phone's 5 s loop makes while its chat is open, in the same private database, so 0.2 requests a second. Apple publishes no per-user request rate for the private database (the figure quoted for CloudKit is 40 a second, TN3162 says only that a throttle answers `requestRateLimited` with a retry after); a throttled read fails that pass, and the next poll reads again. Off the LAN the poll is the way a turn is found: the push (below) arrives late.

## The nudge

A phone on the LAN tells the hub its turn is in the log (`HubNudge`, `Packages/TopoLink`): once its limb's turn is saved under a hub's fresh lease that names an endpoint, it opens the hub's channel (*The channel*, below) at that endpoint's host and the port the hub's key record names, and sends one line, `moved <holder> <epoch> <mark>`, the lease it found and the turn's mark (the eight letters or digits of its nonce the phone's marks carry), and keeps the connection open. The hub runs a pass now (`HubMind.nudge`, trigger `nudge`), exactly as a push does, and answers `read` when the pass that read the log after the nudge has ended, so a reply it wrote is in the log; the phone then reads the log now rather than at its loop's next pass. The hub answers `no` at once, running nothing, when it does not hold that lease (a stale epoch, another hub, a hub whose mind is off, asleep, stopped, starting or holding off), and when 8 nudges already wait (`LeaseProbeServer.nudgesAtOnce`): those already have a pass coming. A `moved` line on the probe's own plain port is answered `no` and runs nothing, whoever sends it. A nudge waits for its pass's end at most 10 min on either side (`nudgeBound`, `HubNudge.answerBound`), every nudge waiting is answered `no` when the mind stops (off, sleep, quit, a halt), and a connection that says nothing is closed after 5 s (`requestBound`). A pass that found nothing for a nudge whose turn is not in the log as read then (the zone's change feed can be behind a save answered a moment before) is run again a second later, three times at most (`hub.nudge.again n=`).

Nothing waits on the nudge. With no LAN between them (the phone on cellular, the Mac on another network, local network access not granted on the phone, a connection refused) the hub finds the turn by its poll or the push, the phone finds the reply by its loop or its push, as they always can; a nudge lost costs at most the poll's interval. The endpoint is the Mac's first IPv4 address on an `en` interface and the listener's port, written into the lease record (`HubIdentity.addresses`), which lives in the private CloudKit database. Off that network the phone's connect fails or runs out within `HubNudge.connectBound` (2 s, the channel's handshake included), and the endpoint is then not tried again for a minute (`HubNudge.Unreached`) unless the phone's network changes (a path monitor's interfaces) or the app comes to the front, so a turn off the LAN pays the 2 s at most once a minute. A bound that runs out after TCP connected is a hub reached whose handshake was slow (`handshakeTimeout`), not a hub off the network: it mutes nothing, and drops the phone's copy of the key, so the next nudge reads the record in case the port is another listener's now; a pass-through framer beneath the channel's TLS (`TCPReached`) is started by Network.framework once TCP is up, which the connection's own state does not say until the handshake has finished.

## The heads-up

A phone that last found this hub answering tells it as the words reach the line, before their save begins (`HubNudge.Coming`, `Harness.headsUp`): it opens the channel and sends `coming <holder> <epoch> <mark>`, the hub's lease as its last limb's turn found it and the turn's mark, and no word. It sends one only when every condition is already in hand with nothing asked: the phone stands elsewhere, the lease it kept names a hub and an endpoint, the key for that epoch is kept from an earlier nudge (`NudgeKeys.kept`, no record read), local network access is allowed (never asked for here), and the words go on an empty line with nothing being sent, so their save begins now. At most one goes for a nonce, the send awaits nothing of it, and a heads-up that fails leaves the turn to go exactly as one with none: the save, then the nudge.

The hub takes it (`HubMind.headsUp`) only when its mind is answering, awake, holds that lease, is running no pass and has none asked for, and holds no other heads-up; it answers `ready` and starts a pass now (trigger `coming`), or `no` with nothing run. On the plain port, `coming` and `saved` are answered `no` in every form, and a `saved` line as the channel's first is too. The pass gets ready while the phone's save is out: it reads the change feed, and on a lease held fresh it sends the lease's renewal at once, then waits for the turn (`TurnRunner.answerPending(coming:)`, `ComingTurn`). Once saved, the phone sends `saved <device>/<sequence>` down the same connection, with ` stream` as a nudge has it, and that line is the nudge of the same lease and mark; the phone keeps only a `read` to it, and on a `no` (the hub's wait ended first) or a connection gone it sends the nudge the usual way; a plain `moved` is unchanged, so a hub built before the heads-up answers `coming` `no` and takes the nudge as ever. The pass fetches the turn by its ref in the probe's one request (`TurnLog.probed(through:)`, read-your-writes where the feed lags a save), with the reply's marker and the model's choice beside it, so the choice and the marker are read after the turn is visible; a turn named that is not there, or not a turn, leaves the read incomplete. The nudge that came down the heads-up's connection is that pass's to answer, and is answered `read` when it ends.

The wait is bounded at 5 s from the heads-up (`HubMind.standardComingBound`, the answering poll; the phone's save is bounded by the lease's 4 s patience), and ends at once on the connection closing, any other line, a stop, a sleep or the switch going off. Another trigger during the wait (a push, a nudge for another turn) has the pass read the feed again and answer what it finds, and wait on for a second at most from then (`ComingTurn`'s `afterOther`), so a trigger whose turn the feed does not show yet is read again by a pass of its own no later than a nudge's own read again a second on would be. A heads-up whose turn never comes ends its pass with nothing asked and nothing written but the renewal. The 8 nudges' places count heads-ups until their connection ends, so a flood is bounded per connection slot, and one needs the key.

**The renewal at the ask.** The brain is asked only on a word from the lease this pass heard: a renewal it sent that answered held within `TurnRunner.askWithin` (0.4 of the heartbeat, N = 2 s at the standard timing) before the ask, or its own claim. A pass that waited longer since its renewal sends another beside the fetch when the first answered more than N/2 ago, and one whose word is older than N at the ask, or whose lease says it is no longer primary (`isPrimary()`, a heartbeat that met a claim), renews again first and asks for the lease the long way if that is refused. Within N of an answered renewal only a deliberate takeover moves the record (another hub's start, the make-primary control, a phone holding a lease it adopted with nobody confirmed); such a takeover landed inside N and not yet met by a heartbeat is not seen at the ask, so the brain may be asked, and the reply's batch, a compare-and-set over the lease version held, is refused with nothing written. A pass without a heads-up asks a moment after its renewal answers; one with a heads-up may ask up to N after it, so that window is wider by up to N, and inside it the mind runs a whole turn, tools included, whose reply is then refused.

## The reply as it is written

A phone's nudge asks for its turn's reply as the mind writes it (`moved <holder> <epoch> <mark> stream`), and, while the menu's "Stream replies to the phone" is on (the default, `stream.replies` in the hub's defaults), the hub sends it down the nudge's connection before it answers `read`: one line of JSON per `ReplyFrame` (`Packages/TopoCore`), `turn` with the refs of the person turns the reply answers, then `message` where a message begins, `text` with the next characters of its words, and `tool` where a tool call begins. They come from Claude Code's partial messages (`MindStream`: `message_start`, a `text_delta`, a `tool_use` block's start, a sub-agent's none), read once the turn is the mind's own (its input's echo), and are handed by `NativeBrain` to `ReplyCast`, which `TimedBrain` tells which turns each reply answers. A nudge is handed only a reply that answers the turn its mark names, from the moment the mind is asked for it, or at once with what it has missed when that reply is being written already (a poll or a push began the pass before the nudge came); everything is handed under one lock, in the order written, and sent at once without waiting on the phone, so a slow or gone phone holds nothing of the mind's. A nudge stops being handed anything when its call returns: its pass's end, or the mind stopping, which answers it `no`. A turn whose answer throws — the process exited, the turn stalled or failed, the mind stopped or slept under it — ends its stream with `cut`, so the phone drops the words and its spoken wait at once rather than when the log goes on (`docs/harness.md`); the turn may still be answered later, in the log (a pass that asks it again, a turn the sleep cut off heard again at the wake, the phone once the lease lapses), and is drawn when it lands; a reply the mind finished and could not save is owed, and comes in the log, so it gets no `cut`. Words handed in one frame are sent as lines of at most 16 KB (`LeaseProbeServer.frameLine`, a quarter of the 64 KB line the phone reads, `HubNudge.frameMaximum`), as several `text` frames split between characters when they are longer: a follower that comes mid-reply is handed each message's words so far in one. Turning "Stream replies to the phone" off stops the streams under way at their next frame (`ReplyCast.gated`), and nothing more of those replies is sent but a `cut`; the phone keeps what was drawn until the log has the reply. What is streamed is provisional and the log's reply, saved under the same nonce as ever, is the truth; the phone's side is `docs/harness.md`. A phone built before the channel nudges the plain port and is answered `no`, so it is sent nothing and finds the reply in the log; a phone with the channel and a hub built before it finds no key record and sends nothing. A frame of a kind the phone does not know is passed over.

**What crosses the network.** The reply's words, and where its messages and tool calls begin, sealed in the channel: no tool's name, input or output, nothing a sub-agent wrote, no person's words, no token. The phone draws a stream only from a channel the hub's key opened, for its own turn's ref, and replaces it with the log's reply. The menu's switch is the owner's choice of whether replies cross the LAN this way at all; off, the phone draws the reply when it lands.

The person's words do not cross the socket: the hub reads the turn from the log as always. The channel authenticates both ends and seals and numbers every record, which is what a line that could start the mind's tool use (permission mode `auto`) would need of it.

## The channel

The nudge and its stream go over TLS 1.2 on a pre-shared key (Network.framework's `sec_protocol_options_add_pre_shared_key`), in the one suite `TLS_ECDHE_PSK_WITH_CHACHA20_POLY1305_SHA256` (`NudgeTLS`, `Packages/TopoLink/Sources/TopoLink/NudgeKey.swift`): each connection's keys come from an ephemeral exchange the pre-shared key authenticates, both ends prove they hold the key in the handshake's Finished messages, and every record is sealed and numbered, so a record changed, dropped, replayed or reordered ends the connection. No certificate is made or read, and a peer that presents one is refused. It is not TLS 1.3: on macOS 27.2 Network.framework completed no TLS 1.3 handshake on an external pre-shared key alone (Apple's pages for these calls say which releases have them and nothing of the versions they work at), while TLS 1.2 completed in this suite on macOS and in the iOS 27.2 simulator. What was configured is not taken as what was agreed (two ends each asked for `TLS_PSK_WITH_CHACHA20_POLY1305_SHA256` alone finished on `TLS_PSK_WITH_AES_128_GCM_SHA256`): each end reads the version and suite off its finished handshake (`NudgeTLS.agreed`) and drops a connection that is anything else, the phone before it sends its line, the hub before it reads one.

The key is 32 bytes from the system's secure generator, minted by the hub at each launch (`NudgeKey.mint`) and kept in memory and in a record of the hub's own in the private zone, `NudgeKey` named `nudge-key/<hub>`, with the name it goes by in the handshake (sixteen hex digits) and the port of the channel's listener, a second listener on a free port, not advertised. The hub saves the record before it makes the mind. A save that fails holds up nothing: the mind is made all the same, a phone finds no key (or the last launch's, which the handshake refuses) and sends no nudge, and the hub's 10 s loop saves the same key again until a save lands; the log line says the failure's kind and never the error's description, which can carry the record, and the status and the menu say the key is not saved. Every connection to the channel whose handshake does not finish in time or agrees anything but the channel's version and suite is counted (`LeaseProbeServer.handshakesFailed`, the mark `hub.channel.handshakeFailed n=<count>`, the status's count): a count alone, nothing of the peer. Only the Apple ID reads that zone, so only its devices hold the key; the record is an ordinary field, as the log's words are, so it is as private as the log and no more (iCloud's server-side keys, not end-to-end). The phone reads the record by ID (`NudgeKeys`, bounded at 2 s) the first time it nudges under a lease epoch, which every start of the hub's mind moves, and keeps it for that epoch; a handshake the hub refuses (a hub launched since, under the same epoch) drops the copy, and so does a connect that failed or ran out (a hub launched since, listening on another port), and the next nudge reads the record again; after a connect the endpoint is muted for its minute first, so a hub off the network costs a read of its record at most once a minute. No log line or mark carries the key: its description names the hub and the key's name alone, and a record printed whole, by itself or in the error that carries it, says `<sealed>` for its secret (`Record.sealedFields`).

**What a LAN peer can do.** One on the path between the phone and the Mac (on the same Wi-Fi, or the router) sees that a phone connected to the Mac's channel and when, the key's name in the handshake, and how many bytes went each way and when, which follows the reply's length and pace, not its words; it reads no frame, no mark and no line, cannot send one on the phone's connection or replay one, cannot pose as the hub to the phone or as the phone to the hub without the key, and learns nothing of the key from a handshake it watched or one it began, the key being 256 random bits. It can drop or delay the traffic, which costs the stream and leaves the reply to the log. One off the path that reaches the Mac's ports can ask the probe's `hold` (whether the hub holds a lease, which the record already says to the Apple ID), send `moved` to the plain port and be answered `no` with nothing run, and begin handshakes on the channel that fail, telling it whether a name is this launch's key's; each connection is closed after 5 s of nothing (`requestBound`, the handshake inside it), and a flood can hold the 8 nudges' places only with the key.

**Who can nudge.** A device on the Apple ID, which can read the key. The listener is on every interface of the Mac, so anything that can reach the Mac's ports can connect, and the handshake is what keeps a nudge out: one without the key runs nothing and is sent nothing. A nudge that has the key causes one pass, a read of the log's change feed (one request), and, only if the log has an unanswered person's turn, the answer the poll would have given within 5 s anyway. A nudge to the wrong listener (a memory-store hub, a port reused by something else) fails its handshake or names a lease that listener does not hold, and runs nothing. The probe's `hold` line is not on the channel: other devices ask it of a lease's endpoint without the key, and it answers only whether the lease is held.

The push is `HubPush` (`Apps/TopoHub/HubPush.swift`): a `CKQuerySubscription` on `Turn` creation in the `Topo` zone, content-available only, `sequence > 0` (for the reason `docs/harness.md` gives for the phone's), saved under its own id, `hub-turn-created`, against the real store only and once a launch with the switch on. The hub never saves or reads the phone's `turn-created`: subscriptions are kept in the Apple ID's private database, so the id is what tells the two apart. The save is judged by its own per-item result for that id, and a save is followed by one read back by id; the `hub` log line says which of `already there`, `saved, and read back`, `saved, but a read by id straight after does not find it` or `saved; the read back failed` it was. `HubAppDelegate` registers for remote notifications at launch (silent pushes ask no permission) and logs whether APNs registered (`push` category, never the token). The pushes arrive on the hub's own topic, `com.apple.icloud-container.zone.hexagon.topo.hub`, through `usernoted` (which logs them as `Received push from topic …` and files each as an undisplayed notification record). Every push the delegate is handed is logged as it arrives (`remote notification: …`, with the kind and subscription id, never the payload; `HubPushKind`, `Packages/TopoMind`), and any CloudKit push of the log's container starts a pass through `HubModel.pushed()`, whatever subscription id it names, since a spare pass costs one read; a push of another container or none of CloudKit's is logged with `; no pass`. The push carries nothing; the turn is read from the log as always.

A content-available push goes at APNs' low priority (5). `apsd` logs each arrival with its priority and the server's send `timestamp`, and the hub's have arrived well after it was sent, often after the 10 s poll had already answered the turn they were for. Whether the push is the fast path is read from `hub.push.received` against the `hub.pass.asked` of the turn it was for, not from the mark appearing.

## Off the home network

The hub asks its router for a public TCP port onto the channel (`HubPortMapping`, mDNSResponder's `DNSServiceNATPortMappingCreate`, which speaks NAT-PMP, PCP and UPnP and renews the mapping while the hub runs) and publishes the external address and port in its key record as `publicEndpoint`, waiting up to 3 s for the router's answer before the key's first save; the run loop republishes a mapping that changes. Only the channel is mapped, since it opens to the Apple ID's key alone; the probe and the surfaces' listener stay on the LAN. A phone opens the channel at every candidate at once (`HubNudge.connect`: the lease's host at the key's port, gated on local network access, and the public endpoint, which is not) and keeps the first whose handshake agrees, marking `nudge.connected via=lan|public` and each candidate that failed (`nudge.candidate <name> <kind>`); the key is dropped only when every candidate failed. A router with no mapping protocol, a home behind carrier-grade NAT, or a network that refuses the outbound connection leaves the phone on the poll and the push as before. The phone reads the key once a lease epoch, so a mapping that changes under a held lease reaches it at the next epoch.

## The mind's home

The mind has none of the Mac user's Claude Code: its working directory is `~/Library/Application Support/Topo Hub/home` and its `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` `~/Library/Application Support/Topo Hub/claude` (`MindHome`), both made at each start. Claude Code reads `CLAUDE.md`, `CLAUDE.local.md` and `.claude/CLAUDE.md` in every directory above the working directory, so from there it would read `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` as a project's memory; `MindHome.prepare` merges a `claudeMdExcludes` entry for each of those, in every directory above, into the config directory's `settings.json` and leaves the rest of the file as it is (a file that is not a JSON object is refused, not overwritten). `claude -p /context` run from the home with that config directory lists no memory files. The process runs with permission mode `auto`, its environment the few variables `NativeBrain` inherits and a `PATH` of Homebrew's prefixes, `~/.local/bin` and the system's (`MindHome.path`); `claude` is `~/.local/bin/claude`.

The login is Claude Code's own, made by hand in that config directory: `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR=~/Library/Application\ Support/Topo\ Hub/claude ~/.local/bin/claude auth login --claudeai`. The menu shows the command when the mind is signed out. Whether it is signed in is `claude auth status --json` with that config directory, run off the main thread with a 20 s bound: the method and the subscription, never the credential.

## The mind's reach

The hub runs a tool service of its own on loopback and installs a `topo` command and a skill for it in the mind's home at each start (`HubTools.install`), on the CloudKit store only. The skill is what the mind knows of everything in this section.

**The phone's tools.** `topo --device iphone <tool> …` asks a device to run one of its own tools. The call and its answer are one record, the target's mailbox `tool-call/<device>` (`RemoteCall`): one slot a device, every write a compare-and-set on the read it follows, so a call is claimed once by the device it names, and one not claimed within 45 s is cancelled and never runs late. The phone hears of a call by the record's silent push (`RemotePushes`), and the hub writes a visible alert for a call still unclaimed after 6 s. With Topo open the phone answers in a second or two; in the background it answers when the system wakes it; iOS sends a force-quit app no silent push, so only the alert reaches that phone. The answer carries how the phone came to run it (`evidence`), which the hub marks as `hub.call.answered <tool> status=<n> via=<push|alert|front|turn> app=<state> claimed=<n>ms`. A device is named by its role record (`topo devices`).

**Words for the person.** `topo notify TITLE [BODY]` writes the hub's one alert record, `alert/<hub>` (`RemoteAlert`), and every device subscribed to it (`alert-said`) is sent its title and body as a visible notification by iCloud, with no device running anything to show it. Each reply the mind saves is said the same way in a record of its own kind, `reply-alert/<hub>`, carrying the reply's first 300 characters (`reply-said`): a phone with Topo in front shows no banner for it, since it shows the reply, and every other state shows the notification. The hub's default `reply.push` set false turns replies' notifications off. A phone registers both subscriptions at launch while signed in. A notification is iCloud's to deliver: the hub's `hub.reply.alert.saved` says the record was written and nothing says it arrived.

**The launch.** The mind adds to its own Claude Code's arguments by writing `launch.json` beside its config directory: `{"arguments": ["--mcp-config", "/path/servers.json"]}` (`MindLaunch`). The flags taken are `--channels`, `--mcp-config`, `--add-dir`, `--append-system-prompt`, `--plugin-dir`, `--settings` and `--dangerously-load-development-channels`, each with one value that is not empty, does not begin with `-` and is at most 8,000 characters; anything else in the document is dropped. The development channels' names go in one value with spaces between and are passed last, since the flag takes every argument after it. The document is read before each turn, and a change replaces the process between turns, never under one (`hub.mind.started … replaced=launch`), resuming the session. A Claude Code that will not start with the arguments is started without them (`hub.mind.launch.refused`). A channel loaded this way delivers nothing to the mind: Claude Code run headless starts no turn for a channel's notification.

## The inbox

Something the mind did not hear from the person reaches it as a turn of the log. `HubMind.hear` appends a person-side turn in the hub's own name whose text begins with `Inbox.heading`, which says the words are signals collected for the person and not typed by them, and asks for a pass; the pass answers it as any turn, and the reply is saved and notified as any reply. It is refused while the mind is off, asleep, not answering, or this Mac does not hold the lease. A reply that is exactly `NOTHING TO REPORT` (`Inbox.nothing`) is saved and not notified. The phone draws such a turn folded: a label, its first line and its size, opened at a tap (`TurnRow`); a build that does not know the heading draws it as a person's turn.

`topo inbox TEXT` is the door, for anything that should reach the mind the moment it happens.

The hub ships no sources. The mind names them in `inbox.json` in its root (`InboxDocument`), per the person's instructions:

    {"sources": [{"name": "mail", "command": "…", "every": "15m", "urgent": false}],
     "flush": "2h", "quiet": {"from": "23:00", "to": "07:00"}}

`InboxService` reads the document afresh every 30 s and runs each source that is due: `/bin/sh -c` with the mind's environment, in the mind's home, bounded at 60 s. A source prints what there is now, one item a line; the service remembers each line by a hash (`inbox-state.json`, mode 600, the newest 2,000 a source) and holds the ones it has not seen. A source's first run is remembered and tells nothing, and a run that exits non-zero tells nothing. Held lines are handed over together, a block a source, once `flush` has passed since the last hand-over and outside the `quiet` hours, which are this Mac's clock's; a new line from a source marked `urgent` takes everything held with it at once, at any hour. Nothing runs while the mind is not answering, and a batch the mind refused is kept for the next tick. A source's name is ASCII letters, digits, `-` and `_`; `every` is 1 min to 24 h and `flush` 5 min to 24 h (2 h where unsaid), written `90s`, `15m`, `2h`; at most 24 sources; a source that breaks a rule costs itself and no other. A source taken out of the document is forgotten. The marks are `hub.inbox.ran <name> status=<n> new=<n>`, `hub.inbox.flushed why=<urgent|timer> sources=<n> lines=<n>`, `hub.inbox.flush.refused` and `hub.inbox.heard chars=<n>`; none carries a line.

## The model

A turn is answered with the person's choice, made on the device they hold: the `ModelChoice` record (`choice/model`, `Packages/TopoTurn`), which a phone writes before it saves a limb's turn whenever its own setting differs from it (`docs/harness.md`), and which a pass with a turn to answer reads before it asks the mind, in the one fetch by ID that completes its read of the log, beside the reply's marker (`TurnRunner.answerPending(since:choosingFrom:)`; a pass with nothing to answer reads neither). The phone saves the record before its turn, and the fetch by ID is read-your-writes, so a pass whose feed holds the turn reads the choice made for it. The menu's picker (Sonnet, Opus, Fable; Sonnet by default, kept in the hub's defaults under `model`) is the model where no device has said; it does not go away. A fetch that fails is a read of the log that failed, and the pass fails with it. The start gives the process the record's choice (its own fetch by ID, the picker when that fails), so the first turn after it does not restart it. Every request carries its model and `NativeBrain` applies `ClaudeModel.effective`, so a Debug build answers with Haiku whatever the choice, and the menu says so; the signed build `scripts/hub-dev-build.sh` makes is Release and unpinned, and answers a debug phone's choice as it stands. The record holds the family's alias, never a debug build's pin.

A model that differs from the resident process's replaces the process before the turn is written, never under one (`NativeBrain.running`): the old process is ended and a new one started with `--resume <session>`, ready once it answers `initialize`, and `hub.mind.started <model> replaced=<model|exited|none> ms=<n>` says what that start cost. A person who switches models every turn pays it every turn; Claude Code's `/model` in the stream is not used. The marks of a pass say which model it asked for and where it came from: `hub.pass.model <model> <chosen|picker>`.

## The menu

The mind's section shows the switch, the picker, one line on where the mind stands (starting, holding off while the phone answers, answering and whether this Mac holds the lease, asleep, or why it stopped, with Try again), the mind's own line (`NativeBrain.describe()`: its process, model and session), and the last pass's outcome, time and trigger. The status line above it says whether this Mac is primary or who is, from the mind's lease and a read of the lease record on the hub's 10 s refresh.

## Timing a pass

Each pass writes `Perf` marks (`perf` category, the `zone.hexagon.topo` subsystem): `hub.pass.trigger <start|push|poll|nudge|coming>`, then, with the milliseconds since the trigger, `hub.pass.read` (the zone's change feed answered), `hub.pass.probed` (the fetch by ID that completes the read answered: each device's next turn, the reply's marker and the model's choice), `hub.pass.waited` (a heads-up's pass heard of its turn: `waited=` the milliseconds it waited, and `saved`, `other` for another trigger, or `gone`), `hub.pass.lease` (the word from the lease the ask is made on, a renewal's answer or the pass's claim, with `age=` the milliseconds it is old at the ask), `hub.pass.asked` with `turn=`, the milliseconds since the newest turn it answers was said, `hub.pass.written` (the input on Claude Code's stdin), `hub.pass.received` (its echo, which Claude Code 2.1.326 writes as the API's first event for the turn arrives, a few milliseconds before `begun`, so the time from `written` is Claude Code's own preparation and the API's wait for its first event together; the session's transcript in the mind's config directory has the moment the request was sent, its `api-request` entry), `hub.pass.begun` (the reply's first message begun), `hub.pass.words` (its first words, after any thinking), `hub.pass.answered`, `hub.pass.saved` (the reply's batch answered), and `hub.pass.end <outcome>`.

Before the brain is asked, a pass with a turn to answer on a lease held fresh makes two requests one after the other, the feed and then the fetch by ID, and sends the lease's renewal beside the second (`TurnRunner.answerPending`), so the ask waits for the slower of those two. The brain is asked only once the renewal has answered, within `TurnRunner.askWithin` of the ask (*The heads-up*), that the lease is still this Mac's (a claim by another device since the last write refuses it, and the pass asks for the lease the long way, `acquire()`, before anything is asked); a marker found means the reply is in the log already and nothing is asked. A pass that asks for the lease the long way (a lease lapsed, a reply owed, or a renewal that was refused or could not be sent) fetches the marker again once its claim has answered, since a reply another device wrote while the lease was asked for is not in the one fetched with the log. A read the hub's own refresh is making when a pass begins (its `Device` read) is waited for, since the zone's mirror reads one at a time (`ZoneMirror`). A push landing is `hub.push.received`, a heads-up taken `hub.coming.received nudge=<mark>`, a nudge `hub.nudge.received nudge=<mark>` (the phone's `turn=` of the same turn, with ` stream` when it asked for the reply as it is written), the first words handed to the phones that asked `hub.stream.first followers=<n>`, a pass run again for a nudge's unseen turn `hub.nudge.again n=<n>`; takeovers, acquires and stops are `hub.lease.takeOver`, `hub.lease.acquire` and `hub.mind.stopped`, each with its outcome's name; a start that held off marks `hub.lease.holdOff.begin` and, before its takeover, `hub.lease.holdOff.end <replied|lapsed|holder|bound> ms=<n>`. The runner's stages are read off the calls it makes through the database and the brain the hub hands it (`TimedDatabase`, `TimedBrain`, `PassClock.swift`), and the lease's age from the runner's request (`BrainRequest.leaseAge`).

With a heads-up the ask is meant to come 0.2 to 0.4 s after the phone's save answers: the `saved` line on the open connection (a few milliseconds on the LAN) and one fetch by ID (about 0.35 s from this Mac), with the feed already read and the renewal already answered while the save was out. That is an estimate from the stages' times without a heads-up, not a measurement: the phone's `turn.limb.saved` and the hub's `hub.pass.asked` on one turn measure it. No mark carries a word of a turn, and no failure's mark carries the strings Claude Code gave.

## Against nothing real

`TOPO_STORE=memory` puts even a signed hub on the in-memory store, and the hub's first log line says which store it is on (`hub store memory`). On that store the mind's home, and its status, are in `~/Library/Application Support/Topo Hub (memory store)`, and its switch is a key of its own, `memory.mind.on` (`scripts/hub-mind.sh --memory`), so a run against nothing real never leaves the real switch on. Three variables and a signal drive a run there and nowhere else (`HubLaunch.controls`, nil on the real store whatever the environment says, which `HubStallTests` holds and a precondition in `HubModel` checks): `TOPO_HUB_CLAUDE` names the `claude` the mind runs (the suites' fake, `Packages/TopoMind/Tests/TopoMindTests/Fixtures/fake-claude`, behind a wrapper that sets its `FAKE_*` variables, since only a few of the hub's reach it), `TOPO_HUB_LIMB_SAYS` writes a limb's turn at launch, `TOPO_HUB_SILENCE` is the silence bound in seconds, and SIGUSR2 posts the workspace's will-sleep and did-wake in turn. No push subscription is saved there. The fixture is not executable in the tree (the suites copy it and set its mode), so the wrapper runs it as `/usr/bin/python3 <fixture>`. The mind's home outlives the in-memory log, and each launch's limb writes its first turn under the same ref, so a ledger left by an earlier run (`claude/mind.json` in that home) can name the new launch's turn as cut off; a run that needs a clean mind removes it first.
