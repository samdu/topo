# The mind on the hub

With the switch on ("Run the mind on this Mac", off by default, kept in the hub's defaults as `mind.on`, which the menu and a shell both set: *From a shell* below), the hub holds the primary lease while the Mac is awake and answers the person's turns from the CloudKit log through a resident native Claude Code (`NativeBrain`). The phone writes its turn to the log as a limb, defers to the hub's fresh lease (`HubLeaseProbe`, `docs/harness.md`) and draws the reply. Off, the hub claims nothing and runs no mind; the pairing code, the device list and the LAN surfaces carry on either way. The loop is `HubMind` (`Packages/TopoMind/Sources/TopoMind/HubMind.swift`), which `HubModel` (`Apps/TopoHub/HubModel.swift`) makes once the probe listener's endpoint is known and drives from the switch, the workspace's sleep and wake, the push and quit.

## The lease

The hub takes the lease from whoever holds it (`PrimaryLease.takeOver()`) once each time the mind starts: the switch turned on, the hub launched with it on, each wake from sleep, and Try again after a stop. Before it, the start holds off while a phone may be answering (`HubMind.heldOff`): while the lease is a fresh lease of a device that is not a hub, and a person's turn at the log's head is unanswered and was said less than `HubMind.standardHoldBound` (10 min) ago, the hub claims nothing and runs no pass, reading the lease (one fetch by ID) and the log every `standardHoldCheck` (2 s), and the menu says the phone is answering. The takeover goes when the head is answered or gone on from (the phone's reply landed), when the lease lapses or names another holder (a phone that went stops renewing, so it is taken over one lease duration and at most one check after its last renewal), or when the turn, or the hold itself, reaches the bound; a lease or log that cannot be read holds, within the same bound. A takeover over a phone's lease with nothing of the person's waiting goes at once. The bound is what a phone that is alive and never answers the turn costs it: the phone's guest bounds a turn's silence (3 min, after which the turn fails and the phone's own loop asks again) and not its length, so a phone renewing its lease is taken to be answering. The loop has not begun while the start holds off, so no pass's `acquire()` claims over the phone meanwhile. A takeover that cannot be sent is sent again, backing off to 30 s, until it is answered. From then on the lease is kept by its own heartbeats, which every answered write starts (5 s, a 10 s duration), and by the runner's writes, each a batch with a heartbeat. Before every pass a lease this Mac does not hold — lapsed after late heartbeats, or taken by a phone — is asked for with `acquire()`, never `takeOver()`. A phone that claimed over the hub's live lease is a holder the hub yielded to at its next heartbeat or batch, and keeps the lease until it lapses: it is not claimed back on a timer, and the two do not flip. A phone's lease the hub has not yielded to is another matter: the phone answers no probe (its lease names no endpoint, so `SocketLeaseProbe` says no), so `acquire()` claims over it on that one probe, as it would over any holder it cannot reach. That is the hub whose own lease lapsed (late heartbeats, a network gap) and which a phone claimed meanwhile: the hub takes it back at its next pass, and the phone yields at its next heartbeat. Every stop is `PrimaryLease.abandon()`, which stops the heartbeats at once and makes any write sent after it hold nothing; the record lapses one duration later and the phone claims it at its next turn or pass.

## Starting and stopping

A start goes in this order, so the lease is never held by a mind that cannot answer: the mind's home is made (`MindHome.prepare`), Claude Code is asked whether it is signed in (`MindLogin.check`), the resident process is started, the runner is made (its writer is this Mac's, read from the log), then the lease is taken over and the first pass runs.

- **Switch off**: the pass under way is cancelled, the lease abandoned, the session waited for, and the process shut down and reaped before the call returns. The runner checks cancellation before each append, and the abandoned lease refuses a batch, so nothing is written after the switch.
- **Will-sleep** (`NSWorkspace.willSleepNotification`): the same, but the process is left; the Mac suspends it with everything else. The notification's handler waits up to 2 s for the stop, so the lease is abandoned before the system goes on to sleep. A turn the sleep cut off is listened to again on the same process at wake rather than asked twice. Nothing runs while asleep, a dark wake's timers included, because the loop is gone.
- **Did-wake** (`NSWorkspace.didWakeNotification`): a start, with its one takeover.
- **Quit** (`applicationShouldTerminate`): as switch off, with the switch kept.
- **Signed out, or a mind that cannot run**: a login that says signed out, a turn that fails `NativeBrainError.notSignedIn`, a process that cannot be started, a ledger that cannot be read, or a home that cannot be made stops the mind as switch off does and leaves it stopped, saying why, until Try again, the switch, or a wake starts it again. The phone answers once the lease lapses. A turn that ends `unresolved` or `failed` is a failed pass, not a stop; an `unresolved` turn waits for the person, as on the phone.
- **A stalled turn** (`NativeBrainError.stalled`, *A hung mind* below) stops the mind in the same way, and the menu, the status and the `hub.mind.stopped stalled silent|tooLong` mark say so. It is not started again on a timer, which would take the lease from the phone only to stall again.
- **`kill -TERM <pid>`**: the hub handles SIGTERM as Quit, so the lease is abandoned at once (no renewal after the signal), the mind's process is torn down and reaped, and the hub exits; if that takes longer than 10 s it exits anyway. The record lapses 10 s after its last renewal, and the phone claims it at its next turn or pass.
- **`kill -9 <pid>`**, or a crash: nothing of the hub runs, so renewals stop with it and the record lapses as above. The mind's process has a watcher beside it (`MindProcess`, a `/bin/sh` in a group of its own reading a pipe only the hub writes), which the hub's end wakes, and which kills the mind's group and every descendant of it at once. Claude Code with its stdin closed carries the turn under way to its end, tools included, before it exits (2.1.325), so without the watcher an orphan would. It holds no lease, can write no record and has nowhere to send a reply. The next start also reaps any resident the last one left (`resident.json` in the config directory: the pid and its start time, so a later process given the same pid is left alone) before it starts its own, since both would write the one session.

## A hung mind

A turn has two bounds, both from when the hub begins listening for it (`NativeBrain.Configuration`): no line at all on Claude Code's stdout for `silenceBound` (3 min, `NativeBrain.standardSilence`), or no result within `turnBound` (30 min, `standardTurn`). Every line counts, whatever it is. The process runs with `--include-partial-messages`, so Claude Code 2.1.325 writes a line for each piece of a reply or of a tool call's input as the model makes it, `system/thinking_tokens` while it thinks, `system/status` while a request waits, and a `tool_progress` heartbeat every 30 s while a tool runs; its longest quiet in a working turn is the wait for a request's first token. Silence is a process that has stopped: wedged, or its event loop stuck. A tool that never returns heartbeats all the while (Bash ends a command at its timeout), and the turn bound is what ends it.

A turn past either bound is given up on: the brain marks its process never to be asked again, the pass ends, the mind halts (the lease abandoned first, then the process torn down), and it stays stopped as above. The turn's record in the ledger says what comes of it: one Claude Code had taken up (its input echoed) is `unresolved`, as one cut off by a process's exit is, since it may have run tools; one never echoed is cleared and asked again by the next request. Nothing of it can be written by the hub: the pass wrote nothing, the abandoned lease refuses a batch, and no reply was finished, so none is owed.

The phone answers it. Its `answerPending` skips only the turns its own brain holds as unresolved, so once the hub's lease lapses (10 s after the halt) it answers the turn at its next turn or pass, under the reply nonce both derive from the log's heads (`TurnRunner.replyNonce(for:)`), whose marker makes a second write of that reply impossible on any device. When the hub starts again after that, the log has moved past the turn and the hub counts it told. A hub started again (Try again, a wake, the switch) before the phone answered holds the lease once more, and the turn waits for the person to ask again, as any cut-off turn does.

## From a shell

The person is usually away from the Mac, and the session driving the hub has a shell and no menu. `scripts/hub-mind.sh on|off|status` drives the switch: `on` and `off` write `mind.on` in the hub's defaults (domain `zone.hexagon.topo.hub`, as `defaults write zone.hexagon.topo.hub mind.on -bool true` would) and send the running hub SIGUSR1, then wait for its status to show the switch applied and print it. The hub follows the defaults (`MindSwitch.follow`) when the mind is made at launch, so a hub launched with the switch on starts the mind; at its 10 s refresh; and at SIGUSR1, which only ever means "follow the defaults now", never a flip. A stopped mind (signed out, stalled) is started again from a shell by `off` then `on`, as Try again does; `on` over a switch already on changes nothing. `status` prints what the menu's mind section says, from `hub-status.json` in the mind's root (`HubStatus`), which the hub writes at every change and refresh: whether the hub is running and its pid, the switch, the login, who holds the lease, the mind's phase by kind, the mind's own line and the last pass. A status whose pid is not a running TopoHub is said to be a stopped hub's, and that pid is never signalled. Nothing in the status is a word of a turn or a reason Claude Code gave: its mind line is `NativeBrain.describe(reasons: false)`, which says a start failed and not why; the menu shows why. The script reaches an unsandboxed hub, the one `scripts/hub-dev-build.sh` builds; a sandboxed build keeps its defaults in its container. Killing the hub by the status's pid is the rollback: `kill -TERM` quits it cleanly, and `kill -9` leaves nothing that answers or holds the lease (*Starting and stopping*).

## Triggers

A pass is `TurnRunner.answerPending`. One runs at a time: on a trigger, and on a poll as the backstop, every 5 s while this Mac holds the lease (`HubMind.standardAnsweringPoll`) and every 10 s while it does not (`standardPoll`). A trigger while the loop waits starts the pass at once; any number of triggers before it begins are that one pass, and any number during a pass are one more pass after it, since the pass may have read the log before their turn landed. A pass with nothing waiting is one read of the log, two requests (the zone's change feed from its token and a fetch by ID of each device's next sequence number), and touches neither the lease nor the brain: the read the phone's 5 s loop makes while its chat is open, in the same private database, so 0.4 requests a second. Apple publishes no per-user request rate for the private database (the figure quoted for CloudKit is 40 a second, TN3162 says only that a throttle answers `requestRateLimited` with a retry after); a throttled read fails that pass, and the next poll reads again. Off the LAN the poll is the way a turn is found: the push (below) arrives late.

## The nudge

A phone on the LAN tells the hub its turn is in the log (`HubNudge`, `Packages/TopoLink`): once its limb's turn is saved under a hub's fresh lease that names an endpoint, it connects to that endpoint, the hub's probe listener (`LeaseProbeServer`), and sends one line, `moved <holder> <epoch> <mark>`, the lease it found and the turn's mark (the eight letters or digits of its nonce the phone's marks carry), and keeps the connection open. The hub runs a pass now (`HubMind.nudge`, trigger `nudge`), exactly as a push does, and answers `read` when the pass that read the log after the nudge has ended, so a reply it wrote is in the log; the phone then reads the log now rather than at its loop's next pass. The hub answers `no` at once, running nothing, when it does not hold that lease (a stale epoch, another hub, a hub whose mind is off, asleep, stopped, starting or holding off), and when 8 nudges already wait (`LeaseProbeServer.nudgesAtOnce`): those already have a pass coming. A nudge waits for its pass's end at most 10 min on either side (`nudgeBound`, `HubNudge.answerBound`), every nudge waiting is answered `no` when the mind stops (off, sleep, quit, a halt), and a connection that says nothing is closed after 5 s (`requestBound`). A pass that found nothing for a nudge whose turn is not in the log as read then (the zone's change feed can be behind a save answered a moment before) is run again a second later, three times at most (`hub.nudge.again n=`).

Nothing waits on the nudge. With no LAN between them (the phone on cellular, the Mac on another network, local network access not granted on the phone, a connection refused) the hub finds the turn by its poll or the push, the phone finds the reply by its loop or its push, as they always can; a nudge lost costs at most the poll's interval.

**Who can nudge.** The listener is on every interface of the Mac, so anything that can reach the Mac's port can send a line: a device on the LAN, or further if the Mac's network routes to it. A nudge is not authenticated, and needs no more: it carries no words and no secret, names a lease anyone on the Apple ID can read anyway, and what it can cause is one pass, which is a read of the log (two requests) and, only if the log has an unanswered person's turn, the answer the poll would have given within 5 s anyway. A forged or repeated nudge is one more read; a flood is capped at 8 waiting and closes a silent connection at 5 s. A nudge to the wrong listener (a memory-store hub, a port reused by something else) names a lease that listener does not hold and runs nothing. The pairing gives no authentication for this: the phone has not scanned a pairing code (`docs/pairing.md`), holds no secret the hub knows, and the hub's key in its `Device` record is a public key for a tunnel that is not built; the probe's `hold` line is unauthenticated in the same way.

The push is `HubPush` (`Apps/TopoHub/HubPush.swift`): a `CKQuerySubscription` on `Turn` creation in the `Topo` zone, content-available only, `sequence > 0` (for the reason `docs/harness.md` gives for the phone's), saved under its own id, `hub-turn-created`, against the real store only and once a launch with the switch on. The hub never saves or reads the phone's `turn-created`: subscriptions are kept in the Apple ID's private database, so the id is what tells the two apart. The save is judged by its own per-item result for that id, and a save is followed by one read back by id; the `hub` log line says which of `already there`, `saved, and read back`, `saved, but a read by id straight after does not find it` or `saved; the read back failed` it was. `HubAppDelegate` registers for remote notifications at launch (silent pushes ask no permission) and logs whether APNs registered (`push` category, never the token). The pushes arrive on the hub's own topic, `com.apple.icloud-container.zone.hexagon.topo.hub`, through `usernoted` (which logs them as `Received push from topic …` and files each as an undisplayed notification record). Every push the delegate is handed is logged as it arrives (`remote notification: …`, with the kind and subscription id, never the payload; `HubPushKind`, `Packages/TopoMind`), and any CloudKit push of the log's container starts a pass through `HubModel.pushed()`, whatever subscription id it names, since a spare pass costs one read; a push of another container or none of CloudKit's is logged with `; no pass`. The push carries nothing; the turn is read from the log as always.

A content-available push goes at APNs' low priority (5). `apsd` logs each arrival with its priority and the server's send `timestamp`, and the hub's have arrived well after it was sent, often after the 10 s poll had already answered the turn they were for. Whether the push is the fast path is read from `hub.push.received` against the `hub.pass.asked` of the turn it was for, not from the mark appearing.

## The mind's home

The mind has none of the Mac user's Claude Code: its working directory is `~/Library/Application Support/Topo Hub/home` and its `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` `~/Library/Application Support/Topo Hub/claude` (`MindHome`), both made at each start. Claude Code reads `CLAUDE.md`, `CLAUDE.local.md` and `.claude/CLAUDE.md` in every directory above the working directory, so from there it would read `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` as a project's memory; `MindHome.prepare` merges a `claudeMdExcludes` entry for each of those, in every directory above, into the config directory's `settings.json` and leaves the rest of the file as it is (a file that is not a JSON object is refused, not overwritten). `claude -p /context` run from the home with that config directory lists no memory files. The process runs with permission mode `auto`, its environment the few variables `NativeBrain` inherits and a `PATH` of Homebrew's prefixes, `~/.local/bin` and the system's (`MindHome.path`); `claude` is `~/.local/bin/claude`.

The login is Claude Code's own, made by hand in that config directory: `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR=~/Library/Application\ Support/Topo\ Hub/claude ~/.local/bin/claude auth login --claudeai`. The menu shows the command when the mind is signed out. Whether it is signed in is `claude auth status --json` with that config directory, run off the main thread with a 20 s bound: the method and the subscription, never the credential.

## The model

A turn is answered with the person's choice, made on the device they hold: the `ModelChoice` record (`choice/model`, `Packages/TopoTurn`), which a phone writes before it saves a limb's turn whenever its own setting differs from it (`docs/harness.md`), and which a pass with a turn to answer reads, one fetch by ID, before it asks the mind (`TurnRunner.answerPending(choosing:)`; a pass with nothing to answer reads nothing). The menu's picker (Sonnet, Opus, Fable; Sonnet by default, kept in the hub's defaults under `model`) is the model where no device has said, or the record cannot be read; it does not go away. The start gives the process the record's choice, so the first turn after it does not restart it. Every request carries its model and `NativeBrain` applies `ClaudeModel.effective`, so a Debug build answers with Haiku whatever the choice, and the menu says so; the signed build `scripts/hub-dev-build.sh` makes is Release and unpinned, and answers a debug phone's choice as it stands. The record holds the family's alias, never a debug build's pin.

A model that differs from the resident process's replaces the process before the turn is written, never under one (`NativeBrain.running`): the old process is ended and a new one started with `--resume <session>`, ready once it answers `initialize`, and `hub.mind.started <model> replaced=<model|exited|none> ms=<n>` says what that start cost. A person who switches models every turn pays it every turn; Claude Code's `/model` in the stream is not used. The marks of a pass say which model it asked for and where it came from: `hub.pass.model <model> <chosen|picker>`, and `hub.pass.model.unread <kind>` where the record could not be read.

## The menu

The mind's section shows the switch, the picker, one line on where the mind stands (starting, holding off while the phone answers, answering and whether this Mac holds the lease, asleep, or why it stopped, with Try again), the mind's own line (`NativeBrain.describe()`: its process, model and session), and the last pass's outcome, time and trigger. The status line above it says whether this Mac is primary or who is, from the mind's lease and a read of the lease record on the hub's 10 s refresh.

## Timing a pass

Each pass writes `Perf` marks (`perf` category, the `zone.hexagon.topo` subsystem): `hub.pass.trigger <start|push|poll|nudge>`, then, with the milliseconds since the trigger, `hub.pass.read` (the runner's read of the log answered), `hub.pass.lease` (the lease's write before the brain is asked), `hub.pass.asked` with `turn=`, the milliseconds since the newest turn it answers was said, `hub.pass.answered`, `hub.pass.saved` (the reply's batch answered), and `hub.pass.end <outcome>`. A push landing is `hub.push.received`, a nudge `hub.nudge.received nudge=<mark>` (the phone's `turn=` of the same turn), a pass run again for a nudge's unseen turn `hub.nudge.again n=<n>`; takeovers, acquires and stops are `hub.lease.takeOver`, `hub.lease.acquire` and `hub.mind.stopped`, each with its outcome's name; a start that held off marks `hub.lease.holdOff.begin` and, before its takeover, `hub.lease.holdOff.end <replied|lapsed|holder|bound> ms=<n>`. The runner's stages are read off the calls it makes through the database and the brain the hub hands it (`TimedDatabase`, `TimedBrain`, `PassClock.swift`). No mark carries a word of a turn, and no failure's mark carries the strings Claude Code gave.

## Against nothing real

`TOPO_STORE=memory` puts even a signed hub on the in-memory store, and the hub's first log line says which store it is on (`hub store memory`). On that store the mind's home, and its status, are in `~/Library/Application Support/Topo Hub (memory store)`, and its switch is a key of its own, `memory.mind.on` (`scripts/hub-mind.sh --memory`), so a run against nothing real never leaves the real switch on. Three variables and a signal drive a run there and nowhere else (`HubLaunch.controls`, nil on the real store whatever the environment says, which `HubStallTests` holds and a precondition in `HubModel` checks): `TOPO_HUB_CLAUDE` names the `claude` the mind runs (the suites' fake, `Packages/TopoMind/Tests/TopoMindTests/Fixtures/fake-claude`, behind a wrapper that sets its `FAKE_*` variables, since only a few of the hub's reach it), `TOPO_HUB_LIMB_SAYS` writes a limb's turn at launch, `TOPO_HUB_SILENCE` is the silence bound in seconds, and SIGUSR2 posts the workspace's will-sleep and did-wake in turn. No push subscription is saved there. The fixture is not executable in the tree (the suites copy it and set its mode), so the wrapper runs it as `/usr/bin/python3 <fixture>`. The mind's home outlives the in-memory log, and each launch's limb writes its first turn under the same ref, so a ledger left by an earlier run (`claude/mind.json` in that home) can name the new launch's turn as cut off; a run that needs a clean mind removes it first.

## Tests

`swift test` in `Packages/TopoMind` runs `HubMindTests` and `HubStallTests` against `InMemoryRecordDatabase` and the fake `claude`, with a probe that confirms every holder, so only a takeover can move a fresh lease: off claims nothing; signed out at the switch claims nothing; on takes over once, answers a limb's turns, and does not take the lease back from a phone that claimed it; off mid-turn writes no reply and leaves no process; sleep stops the renewals and wake takes over once; a turn that finds Claude Code signed out stops the loop and lets the lease lapse; triggers at once run one pass and never two together; a nudge runs one pass and is answered once the reply is in, nudges during a pass run exactly one more after it, a nudge for a turn the log does not have is read again three times and one for a turn it has is not, a nudge for another lease or a mind not answering runs nothing, a nudge waiting when the mind stops is answered no, and with no nudge and no push the poll answers (`HubNudgeTests`); a turn is answered with the picker where no device has said and with the choice once one has, and a pass with nothing to answer reads no choice (`HubModelChoiceTests`); a silent mind is given up on at the bound, its lease left to lapse and its process gone, it stays stopped, the phone answers the turn once and the hub, asked again, writes nothing for it; a stalled turn the hub is asked again before the phone answered waits for the person; a start over a phone that holds a fresh lease with the person's turn unanswered asks nothing and claims nothing until the phone's reply lands, takes over at the bound when it never does, and at the lapse when the phone stops renewing (`HubWakeTests`, with a probe that confirms no phone, as the real one does not); a mind that keeps writing past the silence bound is answered; a turn past its bound is given up on however much it writes; a switch kept on starts the mind and an off from another process (`defaults`) stops it; the real store has no smoke-test controls; the status holds no word of a turn; the watcher kills the mind and what it started when the lifeline closes, and goes with a mind torn down; a resident an earlier hub left is reaped before the next starts, and a record naming another process is left alone. `scripts/tests/hub-mind-test.sh` runs the script against a fake `defaults` and a fake hub.
