# The chat screen

The transcript, the row the next turn is written in, the badge and settings sheet, and the composer. Every value these draw with is a field of `Look` (`Apps/Shared/Look.swift`), and CLAUDE.md's *Where the risk lives* says what a literal in a view costs. Topo on the glass is `docs/mascot.md`; the look as a document in the vault is `docs/memory.md`.

## The transcript

The transcript is `Apps/Client/TranscriptView.swift`, one view drawn by the phone, the watch and the television. Its turns are a lazy stack and the row of the turn on its way sits after it, outside it, so the row is always made: over a transcript taller than the screen, a row inside the lazy stack scrolled to it loads, grows the stack, is scrolled out, unloads and shrinks it again inside one layout pass, which never ends and freezes the app. A drag down the transcript takes the keyboard down with the finger (`scrollDismissesKeyboard(.interactively)`), and so does a tap on the transcript's empty space: a clear layer behind the stack, run the scroll view's height past the last turn, whose tap clears `Draft.typing`. A turn's words, a link in them and the row are in front of that layer, so a tap on any of them is never that tap.

Which side a turn is on and whether it is enclosed is what says who said it: the person's turns sit on the right in a bubble outlined in the theme's primary colour, Topo's on the left as plain text, and the time is under the words on the turn's own side. Topo's turns keep a margin on their trailing side, `look.transcript.replyTrailingInset` (70 points on the phone, tuned on a device so that he can be trapped beside the column rather than derived from his box, read from 0 to 200, none on the watch and the television), which is where he stands beside his replies; the person's turns keep the same room on their leading side, `look.transcript.personLeadingInset` (by default equal to the reply's on every screen, read in the same range), so both sides of the conversation are indented alike, and a bubble wraps within what is left, as does the row being written, which takes the inset only as far as leaves its bubble `look.draft.minimumWidth` and the control its slot (`DraftInset`), so Send stays in the column at the top of the inset's range.

Topo's turns are markdown and the person's are the words as typed. A reply is cut into blocks by `Markdown.blocks` (`Apps/Client/Markdown.swift`) over Foundation's own parse, `AttributedString(markdown:)` with the full syntax, which marks every run with the block it belongs to: paragraphs, headings, list items with their marker, fenced and indented code with its language, rules, and a table as one block of its header row and its rows (`Kind.table`), every row holding a cell for every column — the parse makes no run for an empty cell, nor for a row of them, so each cell is placed by the column and the row the parse names and the gaps are filled — with its column's alignment on it, each block carrying how many lists and how many quotes it sits inside, and how many of those lists sit outside the outermost quote. A single newline stays a line break, as a reply's newlines always have, rather than folding into a space, and beyond that CommonMark's own readings stand (a line's leading spaces go, a tab-indented line is code, a backslash escapes); a source with words in it that parses to nothing is drawn as its plain text; a web link — `http` or `https` with a host, written as a link or a bare URL the parse linked — keeps its address on its own run (`Markdown.opens`) and is drawn underlined in `linkInk`, the one part of a reply that is a tap target: `Text` hit-tests the linked run itself and opens the address through the environment's `openURL`, and nothing here adds a gesture or a region — so the target is `Text`'s own: a touch goes to the nearest glyph, which makes a touch on the run, within a few points of it, or in the blank space after a run that ends its line the link's, and a touch on the words round it nothing; a link to anything else (the app's own scheme, another app's, a file, a script, a relative path) is its words and goes nowhere; a parse that fails is the reply as one plain paragraph. The blocks are kept by the words they were cut from (`Markdown.cached`), since a lazy transcript lays a reply out again each time it scrolls into view. `MarkdownText` (`Apps/Client/MarkdownText.swift`) draws them from `Look.Markdown`: a paragraph in the transcript's own `bodyFont` and `text`, so a paragraph is drawn as words always were; headings in `heading1Font` to `heading3Font`; an item's marker in `marker` and its words `markerSpacing` after it; a quote behind a bar of `quoteBar` and `quoteBarWidth` for each quote it sits inside, each bar `quoteIndent` from the next and from the words, whose ink is `quoteText` whatever block they are, a fence inside a quote keeping `codeInk`; each block led by `listIndent` for every list it is inside, the lists outside its outermost quote leading the bars in and the lists inside it leading only what the bars enclose, so the bars of one quote stand in one column however deeply a list inside it nests — which leaves the inner bar of a quote inside a list inside a quote in the outer bar's column, the one order two counts cannot tell apart; a rule of `ruleWidth`; a table as a `Grid`, its header row in `tableHeaderFont` over a rule of `tableRule` and `tableRuleWidth`, its cells styled inline as a paragraph's are, `tableColumnSpacing` and `tableRowSpacing` apart and aligned as their column is, the grid as wide as its cells and scrolled sideways on the phone with no cell wider than `tableCellMaxWidth` (a sentence in a cell wraps inside its column), and the column's width with its cells wrapping on the watch and the television (`codeOverflow`, as a fence); inline code in `codeFont` and `codeInk`, and a fenced block in the same type inside an enclosure of its own, `codeBlock`, a `Look.Enclosure` like the bubble, under its number in the reply at its trailing edge (`Block.codeNumber`, from 1 in the order the blocks are written) in the type and ink of a turn's time, `labelFont` and `caption` — the number the voice says in the block's place, "See code block 2.", so a listener can find the block meant. An image (`![alt](source)`) is a block of its own (`Kind.image`), drawn after the block it was written in, which keeps its words; one written as a link's words (`[![alt](source)](address)`) is both, the link staying on its alternative text. A reply carries an image as that markdown in its text, and the log holds nothing more. What Claude Code could open, the reply draws: a source that is a path is the guest's own path — absolute (`/tmp/chart.png`, `/home/topo/chart.png`, `/memory/…`) or from its home, `~` included — and the guest is asked for it (`GuestImages`, `Apps/Topo`, over `Guest.contents(ofFile:from:limit:)`): one short program in the guest that reads a regular file of at most 8 MB, bounded in bytes and in time (25 s, longer than the vault's own wait), so the guest's mounts, links and permissions decide what is read and the app opens nothing of its own. A file in the memory is therefore read through the vault's filesystem and its coordination, as any guest read is. A web address (`http` or `https` with a host) is never fetched, and nothing else is an address: a name with a colon in it is a name, for the guest to find or not. The read is awaited off the main actor (`ReplyImage`, the reader in the environment as `replyImages`), and the reads are made one at a time, one for a path however many rows name it, since each holds a thread until its program ends and a reply can name any number of images. What the guest answered — the bytes, or that there was no file — is kept by path for 30 s (`GuestImageStore`), so a row the transcript makes again draws its picture in its first frame. A change in what the guest can read — its home mounted, the memory mounted or unmounted, a sign-out — ends an age: everything kept goes, a read still under way is neither kept nor answered when it lands, and every row reads again (`GuestImages.Mounts`). The images of a block are drawn in the order they were written. One written as a link's words is found by where it was written: the parse says where in the reply each run was (`appliesSourcePositionAttributes`), and a linked run written as `![…](…)` is an image, so the same text in code, or the same address on another link, is none. A file that reads and decodes (ImageIO, no larger than 2048 pixels on its long side) is drawn as wide as the column, no taller than `imageMaxHeight`, its corners `imageCornerRadius`; anything else is the alternative text behind a quote's bar in `quoteText` over the reason in the caption's type and ink — "Not on this device" (the guest has no such file it can read as a picture, or there is no guest: a second device, a watch), "On the web, so not fetched" with the address under it as a link, or "Not a file or a web address". When the voice begins that sentence (`Speaker.cue`, `docs/voice.md`) the chat hands the cue to the transcript: it scrolls the block into view by as little as shows it whole (`ScrollViewProxy.scrollTo` with no anchor, to the block's `CodeBlockCue.Place`, the reply's turn and the block's number), so a block already on the screen does not move, and a block in a row the lazy stack has not made is reached by scrolling its row in first (`TranscriptView.made`) and the block once the row has made it and it says so (`codeBlockAppeared`), wherever in a row of any height — that second scroll a jump rather than a glide, since the stack is still measuring the rows around it and a re-layout during an animated scroll puts the transcript back on the row's top; the block's enclosure pulses (`CodeBlockPulse`, `codePulse`: an outline inside the enclosure's edge in `accent` breathing from nothing to `width` at `opacity` and back, `cycle` seconds a breath — 1.5 by default, a slow breath rather than a flash — two breaths, on a raised cosine, so it never jumps — `Pulse.level`), that block alone: the voice moving on to the next block leaves the one before it at rest, and a block whose row is first made with its cue already set pulses as it appears; and it reports its frame as the one Topo stands beside (`mascotBeside`, `docs/mascot.md`). A block that is only in the transcript is never reached, since only a reply being read aloud makes a cue. Code is monospaced whatever `codeFont` names, since a document's font names no design. A code line longer than the column scrolls sideways inside its enclosure on the phone and wraps on the watch and the television (`codeOverflow`), where the crown and the remote move the transcript and nothing inside it. The person's turn stays literal because the row it is written in is drawn at the size the landed turn will be, which a turn restyled on landing would not be. `MarkdownTests` pins what sources make which blocks, which links stay on their run and which are words, that a table keeps every cell the parse kept in its own column — an empty cell, a table inside a list and inside a quote included — and what awkward ones — an asterisk between numbers, an open fence, a block of HTML — draw as; `MarkdownRenderTests` holds, through a real window under each screen's look, that a fence draws its enclosure and stays in the column, that each fence carries its number over its trailing edge, that a reached block's outline draws nothing at rest and rings its enclosure at the look's width in the look's accent at the peak of each breath, and that the breath is two eased peaks with no jump, that emphasis, strong, strikethrough and inline code draw, and that a heading is taller in Topo's turn and literal in the person's, that a quote draws a bar for each level and its words after the last of them, that a quoted heading and a quoted fence keep theirs, where a bar stands when a quote and a list are interleaved, and that a table draws its header over one rule of the look's width and a row's ink for each row under it, holds an empty cell's column, and wraps a sentence in a cell on every screen. `ReplyLinkTests` (in `TopoUITests`, over the `links` fixture, where a link's tap is recorded on the chat's report and opens nothing) holds the tap target: a tap on the linked word of "see the docs" asks for its address once and a tap on the words before it asks for nothing, and a link wrapped onto a second line is a target on each of its lines and the words before it are not.

Primary is the mind's own colour on both ends of the conversation; `Theme.secondary` is what is neither voice's own turn — a turn a process puts into the transcript, which nothing writes yet and which has no role, and the person's turn while it is still being written — and `Theme.signal` is one on its way, both of them the row at the end of the transcript and neither of them a landed turn.

Holding a turn offers saying it again, or stopping (`Replay`, Topo's turns alone), Copy on any turn, and Edit on one of the person's own, which puts its words back into the field in the glass and raises the keyboard (`TurnActions.edit`, wired in `ChatView`); the log is append-only, so Edit changes what is said next and never the turn that was said. It is offered only while the draft is free to take the words — one holding a turn on its way is drawing the words being sent — and `NextTurn.edit` refuses them over such a turn whether or not the item is there.

Every value the transcript draws with — colour, surface, corner radius, padding, spacing, type — is a field of `Look` (`Apps/Shared/Look.swift`), read from the environment as `@Environment(\.look)`, its defaults chosen per platform and its palette `Theme`; a surface is one of an enum (`glass`, `material`, `flat`) rather than a branch inside a view, and Topo's unenclosed side is a look value of the same type as the bubble (`plain`, set to nothing) rather than a branch either, so the row picks a side and draws no number of its own.

The look itself is a document the mind can write: `look.json` in the vault's root, decoded field by field onto the compiled `Look` by `Apps/Shared/LookDocument.swift` and handed to the environment by `TopoApp`, so every screen of the app is drawn by it.

`Apps/Client/ChatPreviews.swift` is fixture turns, one `#Preview` per platform and `ChatCanvas` — the chat as one view, which the canvas shows and the render suites photograph — all `#if DEBUG`, so the transcript is reviewable with no account behind it and nothing of it ships.

Holding a turn offers what `TurnMenu.items(for:)` says it offers, which is a list of values rather than a block of buttons: a context menu is a gesture no test can press, so which items each role gets is read there and drawn in the row.

## The next turn

The person's next turn is `Draft`, in the same file, handed to the phone alone: the words and whether the keyboard is asked for, both bound to the chat; whether the turn is on its way; whether the pane under the transcript is a row; what sends it; and what takes it back, nil where there is nothing to take back. Its `state` is one of `hidden`, `writing` and `inFlight`, and its `drawer` says where the words are drawn, which is in one place or none: `pane`, the field in the glass, while they are being written and the pane is a row (*The composer*); `row`, `DraftRow` at the end of the transcript, for a turn on its way whatever the pane is and for words with the pane at rest (a caption the microphone is writing, words typed and left when the keyboard was lowered, or words typed with a hardware keyboard in the moment before the pane takes the focus as its row); `none` with nothing to draw. The transcript keeps its end at the words the row draws and at none the field holds (`Draft.followed`), so typing in the field leaves a transcript scrolled back where it was read. So each state is a value a test can make, and the words are never drawn or read twice: a pane at rest neither draws its field nor lets it be read, and the field is empty while a turn is on its way, in either form of the pane: its accessibility value is the words only while it is what draws them (`Draft.fieldValue`), which `DraftRowTests` holds by counting where the words are read, before the keyboard has fallen after a send too. The field holds the draft otherwise, whatever the drawer, and what is typed into it is the draft's, so no letter is dropped or typed over the draft in the moment between the field taking focus and the pane becoming a row (`ComposerGeometryTests` types into a hosted composer in both forms).

`DraftRow` is words and no field, drawn at the size the turn it is about to become will be: a `Text` in an enclosure of `look.bubble`'s shape and `look.transcript.bodyFont`, indented as the person's turn is, so it hugs its words and wraps where the landed turn will. Beside it is a slot (`look.draft.slot`) that is always kept and holds the spinner while the turn is on its way, so the bubble is the same width in both states. A tap on a caption raises the keyboard, and the words are then the field's; holding a turn on its way offers Edit. What the draft has of its own is `Look.Draft`: its two enclosures, the field's minimum width and most lines (`maximumLines`, 5, read from 1 to 20), the room beside the bubble and either side of the field, the slot, and the send's symbol (`sendSymbol`, `paperplane`), type, ink (`sendInk`, the written enclosure's accent) and resting alpha.

The row is not a turn until it lands, and its colour says so: `look.draft.written` while it is being written, `look.draft.sending` while it is on its way, and the person's own `look.bubble` only in the log — three values of one type, each `Enclosure.bubble(screen)` with an accent of its own (`Theme.secondary`, `Theme.signal`), so the row keeps its shape as the turn lands and a look that changes one of the three leaves the other two alone. What else says the turn is on its way is the spinner in the row's slot and a field that is closed: the pane goes back to rest and the keyboard dips until the turn lands, when a keyboard the person had not put down comes back. The row holds one turn, and the line can hold more — the microphone puts heard words in the row over a turn still owed, and a relaunch can find two — so every other entry on the line whose turn the log does not have is drawn as a `QueuedTurnRow`, the person's bubble at the landed turn's place and size in `look.draft.sending`, above the row if it was said before the row's turn and below it if after (`NextTurn.queued(in:)`, off `Harness.unlanded`). Each one is drawn as a landed turn, in `look.bubble`, once the log has it and not before. The transcript scrolls to whatever it draws last (`TranscriptView.end`): a queued turn below the row, then the row, then a queued turn above it, then the newest turn.

The keyboard is the field's: the mark on the resting pane and a tap on a caption set `typing`, the field takes focus from it, and the keyboard going down by any route — the drag, the tap on empty space, another screen — clears it again. The field closing to a turn on its way is not that, and leaves `typing` as the person had it.

## The badge, the jewel and the settings sheet

The chat's chrome is one mark: `Apps/Client/TopoBadge.swift`, a cabochon filling the control at the navigation bar's trailing edge with the octopus cut into it, where the title said "Topo" twice over. A tap opens the settings sheet and a hold opens the diagnostics, which the chat also presents itself so a screen that will not draw is still reachable. The bar's middle carries the chat's notice, out of the composer's way, one at a time (`ChatNotices.Said.notice`): the last failure in `look.transcript.trouble` first, then the turn in flight and where it is ("Reaching iCloud…", "Asking Sonnet…"), then a turn that went to another device's primary ("Saved. Another device will answer here."), which the next turn's progress replaces; in the transcript's `noticeFont`, two lines at most, truncated at the tail past that. The bar holds two lines beside the badge, so `look.json` sets `noticeFont` no larger than `Look.Transcript.largestNotice` (15 points: a larger size is drawn at 15, a larger style refused) and the notices follow the text-size setting no further than `ChatNotices.largestType`. The bar is above the transcript's frame, so Topo never stands under them. `StatusNoticeTests` holds each of the three in the bar, in the harness's words, and that two at once show as one, off `TOPO_DEBUG_NOTICES` (`busy`, `busy-long`, `error`, `info`, `busy-info`, `error-busy`), at the default look and at a 400-point `noticeFont`. The controls that send a turn again stay under the transcript. The badge has one state, because nothing on the phone measures standing: `RoleSelector` holds the role decided at launch and `PrimaryReader` reads a lease's freshness, and neither says whether the mind can be reached.

`StainedGlass`, the cabochon it is cut from, is handed a `Look.Jewel` and a diameter and draws nothing of its own: the body is a slice of jelly-teal glass (`agate`, a 512px disc in the iOS asset catalogue, a circular window onto `Design/topo-jelly.png` cut by `Design/make-icons.swift`, which grounds the app icons on the same stone and cuts the mark into them through the same `Pressed` treatment — so the gem under the thumb and the icon on the home screen are one object), filled as an `ImagePaint` so the light cut into it stays an inner shadow, under a cast — a colour at an alpha in a blend mode — so a cabochon of another colour is three values and never a second picture. The badge's is the stone under `Theme.secondary` (`look.badge.jewel`), the microphone's the same stone with no cast on it. The diameter is handed over rather than read off a `GeometryReader`, because a paint is scaled to the shape it fills and a reader resolves on a second layout pass.

The mark is pressed into the stone rather than laid on it: one treatment, `Pressed`, handed `look.press` — the wall's width as a share of the jewel's diameter (6/512, what the exploration measured), the dark wall above a stroke, the light one below it, and the shade over the floor — with the floor of the cut filled from the same stone aligned to the same jewel. It reads only the alpha of the mark it is handed, so the octopus's drawing and `mic.fill` go through the one treatment, and it fades that mark to nothing rather than hiding it, since a hidden view leaves the accessibility tree and the microphone is the element both UI suites find.

`Apps/Client/SettingsView.swift` is the sheet: a `Form` whose sections have no headings, each row named for what is behind it: first where Topo sits (`PlacementSection` in `Apps/Client/Tuning.swift`, this device's hand on the look: a picker of the three placements, while he is pinned the pin, and a Reset), then Vocabulary, Memory (where the vault's folder lives, `MemoryView`) and Connections, then Diagnostics and About, then Sign out, destructive, and in a debug build, last and under its own heading, Tuning (`TuningSection`: a slider each for `mascot.clearance`, `transcript.replyTrailingInset`, `transcript.personLeadingInset`, `mascot.scale` and `mascot.roamSpeed`, labelled with the value worn, and `Show his field`, the overlay of why Topo stands where he stands (`docs/mascot.md`), a defaults key of this device's (`topo.debug.mascotField`) rather than part of the override. The model and the mute are in the chat's navigation bar (*The composer*). What they set, and what a drag on Topo sets, is a `look.json` of the same shape kept in this device's defaults (`topo.tuning`) and decoded onto the vault's look by `LookDocument` in `wearing`, in every build, so its ranges and per-field fallbacks are the document's and it outranks the vault's `look.json` — a release build reads only the placement and the pin from it, having no sliders, so slider values a debug install left are neither worn nor kept (`Tuning.knobsWorn`); any field of the look the mind in the guest set through `topo look set` (`Apps/Topo/Tools/LookTool.swift`, `docs/guest.md`, *The tool service*) is kept beside it under its own key (`topo.tuning.mind`), judged by `LookDocument`'s reader before it is kept at all, worn in every build since it is the mind's hand and not a slider, and one value with a slider of the same field — whichever was set last is kept and the other dropped; Reset removes both. A debug build launched with `TOPO_DEBUG_TUNING` sets the override, or with it empty removes it, and takes the mind's fields away either way, before anything reads it (`DebugRun.tuning`). The chat's debug report carries the clearance, the placement and the pin worn and what the override holds of the placement and the pin (`DebugRun.ChatReport`), which `TuningSliderTests` in `TopoUITests` reads with a slider moved, with a pin kept and after Reset, and with the glass chosen in the picker).

Signing out is `SignOut`, a value the chat builds from the five things it ends — the speaker, the harness, the memory, the connections, the login, in that order, the harness awaited so what the guest kept of the conversation is gone before the login is — and hands to the sheet, so which of them end, and when, is what `SignOutTests` reads rather than a block inside a button.

## The composer

The bar under the transcript is `Apps/Client/Composer.swift`: a floating pane of glass sitting off the bottom edge, in one of two forms. At rest it is `look.composer.widthFraction` of the screen wide, with the microphone set into a well cut through the middle of it, the control for everything else (`composer-more`, "More", `flank.more`, which opens nothing yet) on the leading flank and the way to the keyboard ("Type instead", `flank.keyboard`) on the trailing one, each in the middle of its flank's inner half so they stand the same distance from the microphone. With the keyboard up it is a row, `look.composer.typingWidthFraction` of the screen wide (0.93) and no wider than the transcript's column, but never narrower than what holds the well, the control, the field at `look.draft.minimumWidth` and the send side by side (`ComposerPlan.floor`) and never wider than the screen (`ComposerGeometry.width`): leading to trailing the well and its jewel at their compact size, the well's middle `jewelInset` from the pane's leading end; the control for everything else; the field the turn is written in ("What to say"), in `look.draft.written`'s enclosure and the transcript's type; and the send ("Send"), a borderless `look.draft.sendSymbol` in `sendInk`, disabled at `sendRestingOpacity` while nothing but space is written. The inks say whose each is: the jewel and the control are Topo's, the field and the send the message's.

The glass is what says the microphone is open, because the thumb that opened it covers the jewel: the pane takes Topo's colour, the jewel goes pale, a glow spills onto the transcript behind, and what is beside the microphone goes to `flank.heldOpacity` while keeping its space, so the pane never changes size under the hand. The mark is `mic.fill`, `waveform` while the microphone is open hands-free and `stop.fill` while Topo is speaking, cut into the stone through the same `Pressed` the badge's octopus goes through, with `look.composer.glyph.openCast` laid over the floor of the cut while the microphone is open — a mark cut into pale stone has no ink to flip. A press that would be refused drains the colour out of the jewel and fades it, the diagnostics `speech` row saying why. The jewel is `StainedGlass` again, handed `look.jewel` at rest and `look.composer.openJewel` — the same stone under a milky veil — open, so both states are values and neither is a branch in a view; every other value the pane, the etch, the well and the mark are drawn with is a field of `Look.Composer`.

`Composer.MicState` is the mapping: the chat hands over `canListen`, `listening`, `owner` and `handsFree` read off `VoiceInput` and `speaking` read off `Speaker`, and the state says which of idle, held, hands-free, dimmed and stop they are, so what the glass draws is a value a test can make. It asks the owner before the manner of opening, so a session another surface holds leaves this glass alone however it was opened. While a reply is being read — spoken or Say it again — the button is Stop, on the resting stone and never dimmed, since stopping needs no ear; the one exception is this screen's own open microphone, which keeps its state, because the next press on it is the one that sends. The gesture is the chat's, `onLongPressGesture(minimumDuration: 0, maximumDistance: 60)` on the whole of the well, handing the press and the state the composer drew the button in to `MicPress.gesture`, which is the whole of the screen's part in a session. The press is what was drawn, not what the speaker says by the time the callback runs, and every decision is made in the callback, in the order the callbacks come; only the call into `VoiceInput` is left to a task: a press on Stop stops the reply and opens nothing, and its release is that press's own and reaches no session either, though the button is the microphone again by then; a press on the closed microphone stops a reply still being read and goes to `VoiceInput`, and one on the open microphone goes to `VoiceInput` alone, since nothing is read while it is open (`docs/voice.md`) and a reply that waited for it is read once the press closes it — the button then becomes Stop. The accessibility labels are `VoiceInput`'s state in words, or "Stop speaking", and they are the only route `MicrophonePressTests`, `TopoOnTheGlassTests` and `SpokenTurnTests` have to the button.

Both forms are one layout over the same views (`ComposerRow`, a `Layout` that places each by its `ComposerPart`), so the field is one `TextField` that is never taken out of the window: a second field in the other form would be a field the keyboard falls between. The send is in the layout only as a row and the way to the keyboard only at rest; the field is in both, not drawn or pressed at rest (the system's text view stays in the accessibility tree there, where activating it is a way to the keyboard); at rest it keeps the trailing flank, so it grows out of the way to the keyboard. Where everything stands is `ComposerPlan`, a pure function of the look, the pane's width and the sizes of what it holds. As a row no control is drawn past the pane's end or over its neighbour whatever the look gives, and where that leaves the field under `look.draft.minimumWidth` the look's values yield in order — the spacing, then the jewel's inset, then the room either side of the field (`ComposerPlan.Columns`); the slots yield last, to a field still under its minimum and only down to a control's reach (`Columns.reach`, 44 points), and in a row narrower than the well and both slots they share what is past the well, the smaller keeping its own where that is no more than half, so the three stay in order and inside the row. The control and the send are each laid out in the slot the plan gives it and no larger (`Cut`), and clipped to it, so a mark larger than its slot is cut at the slot's edge and drawn over nothing. The field's enclosure keeps the look's padding wherever the field has the room, and never more than a quarter of the field's width or height on each side (`Inset`), so the words have half of each. At rest a mark's middle is never nearer the pane's end than half its slot, so a spacing wider than the pane puts neither mark off it. `ComposerPlanTests` holds the order and the well's trailing edge at every end of the ranges the row's fields are read in, and each field of the row's own at each end on a 320-point screen; `ComposerGeometryTests` hosts the row at 320 points at each of those ends and reads the bounds each control is drawn in (`ComposerFrames.Parts`). The row is as tall as the taller of the short well in its insets and one line of the field, so a field of one line stands level with the well; what is written past a line grows the field (`axis: .vertical`) and the pane upward, the well, the control and the send staying on the bottom line. The field takes `look.draft.maximumLines` lines (1 to 20) and no more than the room holds; past that its frame stops growing and every word is kept in it (`ComposerPlan.lines`). The room is the chat column's (`ChatColumn`, which measures it and hands it to the pane): the height the column is offered from under the navigation bar to the keyboard, read from a `GeometryReader` round the column and not from the column as laid out, which a pane that outgrew it would push out; less the line under the transcript that sends a turn again and the memory's offer card over the pane where either is there (`ChatColumnRoom.left`). The card is drawn only while the keyboard is down: with the keyboard on screen the column holds the transcript, the line and the pane, and an offer not yet answered is back when the keyboard goes. The lines leave a row's height of that to the transcript, and the row itself is held to it (`ComposerPlan.row`'s `most`, the room less the room kept under the pane and never under `Well.pressable`; that room is `bottomPadding`, and in a row no more of it than leaves the well that reach): a line of type, a mark, an inset or a slot taller than the room is a row as tall as the room, cut at its own edges (`RowCut`), so the pane never grows up behind the bar. `ComposerGeometryTests` hosts a draft of the most lines on a 320 by 568 screen with a keyboard's room, the column with the card, the line and both, and the row at the tallest type, padding, marks, slot and inset the reader takes, each no taller than the room, and at the accessibility text sizes, the largest with the card offered and the line shown over a keyboard among them; `DraftRowTests` holds it in the running app under the bar.

A press on the microphone while the pane is a row and no turn is on its way is dictation into the draft and sends nothing (`Dictation`, `Apps/Client/Dictation.swift`; `Dictation.beginning` settles which). With a turn on its way it is a spoken turn whatever form the pane is in, since the pane is still a row for a moment after a send and words written after a turn in flight could not be sent and would go when it landed. In a dictation what is heard is written after what was there when the press began, with one space between, and the send in the glass sends it as a typed turn. Which a session is — a spoken turn or dictation — is settled by the press that opens the microphone (`Dictation.begins`) and kept to the session's end, whatever the pane has become by the release. A dictation writes only into a draft that is as it left it (`Dictation.standing`): typing into the draft while the microphone is open over it ends the session with what is in the field kept, and words typed while a release is being heard are not written over. A caption is written into the row only while no turn is on its way (`NextTurn.caption`), and a release that heard nothing leaves a turn on its way in the row (`NextTurn.endCaption`). The whole pane is off limits to a roaming Topo (`docs/mascot.md`), at every presence and in both forms; a Topo placed on the glass stands on it.

The mute and then the model are in the chat's navigation bar, at its leading edge, each an item of the bar's own, so neither shares the other's glass (`Apps/Client/ChatBar.swift`; from iOS 26 a fixed `ToolbarSpacer` stands between them, since the bar draws neighbouring items on one piece). They are drawn in `Look.Bar` (`look.bar`: `font`, a size drawn no larger than `Look.Bar.largestFont`, 22 points, and a larger style refused, since the bar is a fixed height; `ink`) with the flank's symbol names, following the text-size setting no further than the notice does (`ChatNotices.largestType`). The model (`chat-model`, "Choose the model" or "Close the model slider", `flank.models` and `flank.modelsOpen`, its accessibility value the chosen model's name) opens the model slider and shuts it. The slider (`ChatBar.Slider`, `chat-models`) is on a glass of its own (`ChatBar.Drop`) that comes down from under the bar's leading edge while it is open, over the top of the transcript: a line with a stop for each model the chat offers (`chat-model-<alias>`, selected for the one chosen), smallest first, each under the name the look gives it (`look.mind`), and a knob on the one chosen. A tap on a stop or its name chooses it, and so does a finger drawn along the line, at the stop it is nearest; a choice is `Harness.model`, which reaches the guest as `docs/harness.md` says. It is drawn from `look.bar.slider` (`width`, the most it takes, 96 to 560 points, and it is as narrow as the screen leaves it; `height`, 24 to 44; `padding`, between it and the edge of its glass, 4 to 24; `drop`, between the bar and its glass, 0 to 24; `inset`, from each end to the end stops, 8 to 80 and never more than a quarter of its width; `track`, `stop`, `knob`; `restOpacity`, `restLabelOpacity`; `labelFont`, a size drawn no larger than `Look.Bar.Slider.largestLabel`, 14 points; `labelSpacing`), clipped to its own frame, so no value of the look's draws any of it off its glass. A model's name is on one line no wider than its stop's column, cut short where it is longer, and its line is kept whole inside the slider: it is measured, and the knob and then the gap under it are drawn no larger than the slider's height leaves over that line (`ChatBar.Stops.fitted`), with an end stop no nearer the slider's end than half the knob. Where its stops are is `ChatBar.Stops`, one value the slider is laid out by, a finger on the line is judged by and Topo is put by: the slider reports its chosen stop's frame on the screen, down to the foot of its glass, and while it is open Topo hangs under that stop, wearing the head of the model chosen (`docs/mascot.md`). A press on the microphone shuts it. The mute (`chat-mute`, "Mute replies" or "Read replies aloud") is one control with two marks, both laid out and one drawn: whether a spoken turn's reply is read aloud (what muting ends is `docs/voice.md`). The notice (`ChatNotices`) is the bar's principal item, in the room between the controls and the badge, which on a 402-point phone is about 196 points: a turn in flight is a spinner and one text, the status and then the turns behind it ("· 2 waiting") in the caption's colour, so the count wraps with the status. A notice whose words do not fit its two lines there is scaled down, as far as `look.transcript.noticeLeastScale` (0.65, read from 0.5 to 1), before it is truncated; `ChatNoticesRenderTests` holds, as arithmetic, that every notice the harness writes fits two lines at that least size at the largest `noticeFont` in the room a 390-point phone leaves; `StatusNoticeTests` (in `TopoUITests`) holds the running bar to it: a notice whose words take more than two lines at its font is drawn shorter than two lines of that font, so scaled before it is truncated, and the open slider leaves the notice in the bar, above it. `ChatBarTests` holds the look's names, the mute's default, the stops' arithmetic at the ends of the look's ranges, where Topo hangs under a stop and his glides there, along the line and home, and that the chat's debug report carries the model set (`DebugRun.ChatReport.model`) apart from the model a request carries for it (`effectiveModel`, the pin in a debug build); `BarControlsTests` (in `TopoUITests`) presses them in the running app: the mute changes state and back, the two controls stand apart, each stop pressed is the model the control says and the report's `model`, with `effectiveModel` the pin after every choice and Topo standing under that stop, the slider chooses over the keyboard, by a press and by a finger drawn along it, with what is written left in the field, a press on the microphone shuts it, and at each end of `look.bar`'s ranges the controls are inside the bar and the open slider under it, inside the screen's width and above the microphone.

The pane's surface is drawn at a presence, 0 to 1 (`Composer.presence`): nothing over the empty end of the transcript, where glass over a flat background is a lens with an edge and nothing behind it, and the pane whole where turns run under it. The chat works it out from the transcript's scroll geometry (`PanePresence.of`, a pure function beside `MicState`), 0 while the content's bottom edge is above the pane's top and rising over `look.composer.presenceRise` points of content running under it, in `look.composer.presenceDuration`; an open microphone is 1 whatever the geometry, since the tinted pane is what says the microphone is open, and so is the keyboard on screen, since the pane is a row under it and a field on glass that is not there is a field floating, and so is Topo placed on the glass (`holdsTopo`), since a Topo on invisible glass is a Topo floating. For the presence the keyboard is the pane's field holding focus, which the composer reports to the chat (`Composer.focused`), and not `NextTurn.typing`, which outlives the field while a turn is on its way; focus changes in a transaction of its own ahead of the keyboard, so the surface fades in where it stands and then rides up with the pane. The two edges are measured in one named space on the chat, the content's bottom from `onScrollGeometryChange` (iOS 18) and both top edges from `onGeometryChange`, so the offer card and the keyboard's rise move the pane's edge rather than being assumed away. What the scroll geometry gives is the content's y at the top of the scroll view's frame, which reaches under the navigation bar; the top inset comes off to put it at the transcript's own edge, and the stack's trailing padding comes off with it, since those points are content that draws nothing and a pane over them is a pane over nothing. On iOS 17, the deployment target, there is no scroll geometry and the presence is 1, one of the screen's two availability branches (the other is Topo's reading of a reply's lines, `docs/mascot.md`).

The bar itself is `safeAreaInset(edge: .bottom)` on every OS: the transcript scrolls under the pane while there is more to scroll and at rest the last turn stops above it, and what says whether anything is behind the pane is the presence and not the modifier. `safeAreaBar` is not used — on a device it draws a bar background of its own behind the lozenge, a second slab wider than the pane.

In the row the microphone is short: `ComposerGeometry.of` (a pure function beside `PanePresence`) draws the well, the jewel and the mark cut into it, and the room above and below the row, at `look.composer.compactShare` of their resting size (⅔, read from 0.5 to 1). The well is never drawn under `Look.Composer.Well.pressable` (44 points, not a field, since it is the floor a look may not go under) for the row, and the jewel is never drawn bigger than its well in either form. The microphone is drawn at its resting size and scaled as one piece, with the gesture and a `contentShape(Circle())` on the frame as drawn, so a press lands on the short well. The pane's corner radius goes short with it (`ComposerGeometry.cornerRadius`, the resting radius at the same share). `ComposerGeometry.width` is the pane's width in each form.

Which form the pane is in is `ComposerForm.isRow`: the keyboard on screen, or the field holding focus with none. The keyboard is its own safe area: the chat's body is a `GeometryReader` over the whole screen, and the bottom inset past the screen's own (`KeyboardInset.isUp`, against the same inset read with the keyboard's region ignored) is the keyboard on screen. That is read in the transaction the keyboard's rise and fall is laid out in, and nothing in the composer animates the form on a time of its own, so the pane widens and goes short, and back, on the keyboard's own curve, reaching its end with the keyboard, and the transcript is laid out once for both. A hardware keyboard, or one floating over a pad, raises nothing the safe area says, so focus alone is the row too — but a keyboard that is going to rise does so 50 to 80 ms after the field takes focus, and a pane that took the focus at once would change form ahead of the keyboard and off its curve. So focus alone is the row only once it has been held `ComposerForm.patience` (250 ms) with no keyboard come, on `look.composer.duration`, there being no keyboard's curve to move on. A keyboard on screen ends that (`ComposerForm.alone`), however late it came, and a wait that ran out as the keyboard arrived counts for nothing: a pane still a row for the focus alone when the keyboard fell would stay a row through the keyboard's transaction and go to rest off its curve when the field let go. A keyboard that leaves a field still being typed in (a hardware one connected, one set floating over a pad) puts the pane to rest in the keyboard's transaction with the field still holding focus, and the pane takes that focus as its row again `patience` later; the field keeps its touches while it holds focus (`allowsHitTesting`), because a text view whose touches are taken away gives up the keyboard. A headless simulator has no hardware keyboard, so no UI suite reaches the focus-alone path: `ComposerGeometryTests` holds `isRow`, types into a hosted composer at rest, and puts the pane to rest under a focused field, which keeps its focus and what is typed then; the running app with a hardware keyboard is a check on a device.

The composer publishes the pane's bounds and the well's as anchors (`ComposerFrames`, and again into `MascotScene` for Topo). `ComposerGeometryTests` holds, as arithmetic at every end of the ranges of the share, the well, the jewel and both insets together, the short well at or over the floor, the jewel inside it and the corner radius the resting one at the same share; and, reading those anchors in a 320-by-568 window, the well inside the pane in both forms, in the middle at rest and the look's inset from the leading end as a row. `ComposerPlanTests` holds the plan: the row's width and the column's cap on it, every part inside the pane and none over its neighbour at the look's defaults and at the ends of the ranges the row's fields are read in, the order the look's values yield in, the field's growth upward with the bottom line kept, and the resting flanks. `ComposerRenderTests` renders both forms and holds what is drawn and read in each; `DictationTests` holds where what is heard goes; `DraftTests` holds the draft's states and its drawer. `DraftRowTests` (in `TopoUITests`, launched with `TOPO_DEBUG_SOFTWARE_KEYBOARD` (`DebugRun.softwareKeyboard`), since a simulator starts with the Mac's keyboard connected and under it no keyboard rises) raises the keyboard with the mark and holds the row's order, level and width, types on the keyboard's own keys, grows the field to the look's lines, sends from the glass with the words drawn once on their way and the field closed, comes back from a kill to the row the words were sent from, and lowers the keyboard by a tap on empty space and a drag and by neither on a turn's words; `TopoOnTheGlassTests` holds the microphone at two thirds of its size in the row and presses the short well at its edge.
