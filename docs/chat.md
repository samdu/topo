# The chat screen

The transcript, the row the next turn is written in, the badge and settings sheet, and the composer. Every value these draw with is a field of `Look` (`Apps/Shared/Look.swift`), and CLAUDE.md's *Where the risk lives* says what a literal in a view costs. Topo on the glass is `docs/mascot.md`; the look as a document in the vault is `docs/memory.md`.

## The transcript

The transcript is `Apps/Client/TranscriptView.swift`, one view drawn by the phone, the watch and the television. Its turns are a lazy stack and the row being written sits after it, outside it, so the row is always made: with the keyboard up over a transcript taller than the screen, a row inside the lazy stack scrolled to it loads, grows the stack, is scrolled out, unloads and shrinks it again inside one layout pass, which never ends and freezes the app.

Which side a turn is on and whether it is enclosed is what says who said it: the person's turns sit on the right in a bubble outlined in the theme's primary colour, Topo's on the left as plain text, and the time is under the words on the turn's own side. Topo's turns keep a margin on their trailing side, `look.transcript.replyTrailingInset` (110 points on the phone — his box at a scale of 1, one clearance and what his reach keeps from the screen's edge in either facing, less the column's padding, rounded up to ten — read from 0 to 200, none on the watch and the television), which is where he stands beside his replies; the person's turns keep the same room on their leading side, `look.transcript.personLeadingInset` (by default equal to the reply's on every screen, read in the same range), so both sides of the conversation are indented alike, and a bubble wraps within what is left, as does the row being written, which takes the inset only as far as leaves its bubble `look.draft.minimumWidth` and the control its slot (`DraftInset`), so Send stays in the column at the top of the inset's range.

Topo's turns are markdown and the person's are the words as typed. A reply is cut into blocks by `Markdown.blocks` (`Apps/Client/Markdown.swift`) over Foundation's own parse, `AttributedString(markdown:)` with the full syntax, which marks every run with the block it belongs to: paragraphs, headings, list items with their marker and how many lists they sit in, quotes, fenced and indented code with its language, rules, and a table as a paragraph per row with its cells joined. A single newline stays a line break, as a reply's newlines always have, rather than folding into a space, and beyond that CommonMark's own readings stand (a line's leading spaces go, a tab-indented line is code, a backslash escapes); a source with words in it that parses to nothing is drawn as its plain text; a link is its words and goes nowhere, since nothing a reply says is a tap target; a parse that fails is the reply as one plain paragraph. The blocks are kept by the words they were cut from (`Markdown.cached`), since a lazy transcript lays a reply out again each time it scrolls into view. `MarkdownText` (`Apps/Client/MarkdownText.swift`) draws them from `Look.Markdown`: a paragraph in the transcript's own `bodyFont` and `text`, so a paragraph is drawn as words always were; headings in `heading1Font` to `heading3Font`; an item's marker in `marker` and its words `markerSpacing` after it, each block led by `listIndent` for every list it is inside; a quote behind a bar of `quoteBar`; a rule of `ruleWidth`; inline code in `codeFont` and `codeInk`, and a fenced block in the same type inside an enclosure of its own, `codeBlock`, a `Look.Enclosure` like the bubble. Code is monospaced whatever `codeFont` names, since a document's font names no design. A code line longer than the column scrolls sideways inside its enclosure on the phone and wraps on the watch and the television (`codeOverflow`), where the crown and the remote move the transcript and nothing inside it. The person's turn stays literal because the row it is written in is drawn at the size the landed turn will be, which a turn restyled on landing would not be. `MarkdownTests` pins what sources make which blocks, and what awkward ones — an asterisk between numbers, an open fence, a block of HTML — draw as; `MarkdownRenderTests` holds, through a real window under each screen's look, that a fence draws its enclosure and stays in the column, that emphasis, strong, strikethrough and inline code draw, and that a heading is taller in Topo's turn and literal in the person's.

Primary is the mind's own colour on both ends of the conversation; `Theme.secondary` is what is neither voice's own turn — a turn a process puts into the transcript, which nothing writes yet and which has no role, and the person's turn while it is still being written — and `Theme.signal` is one on its way, both of them the row at the end of the transcript and neither of them a landed turn.

Holding a turn offers saying it again, or stopping (`Replay`, Topo's turns alone), Copy on any turn, and Edit on one of the person's own, which puts its words back into the row at the end of the transcript and raises the keyboard (`TurnActions.edit`, wired in `ChatView`); the log is append-only, so Edit changes what is said next and never the turn that was said. It is offered only while the row is free to take the words — a row holding a turn on its way is drawing the words being sent — and `NextTurn.edit` refuses them over such a turn whether or not the item is there.

Every value the transcript draws with — colour, surface, corner radius, padding, spacing, type — is a field of `Look` (`Apps/Shared/Look.swift`), read from the environment as `@Environment(\.look)`, its defaults chosen per platform and its palette `Theme`; a surface is one of an enum (`glass`, `material`, `flat`) rather than a branch inside a view, and Topo's unenclosed side is a look value of the same type as the bubble (`plain`, set to nothing) rather than a branch either, so the row picks a side and draws no number of its own.

The look itself is a document the mind can write: `look.json` in the vault's root, decoded field by field onto the compiled `Look` by `Apps/Shared/LookDocument.swift` and handed to the environment by `TopoApp`, so every screen of the app is drawn by it.

`Apps/Client/ChatPreviews.swift` is fixture turns, one `#Preview` per platform and `ChatCanvas` — the chat as one view, which the canvas shows and the render suites photograph — all `#if DEBUG`, so the transcript is reviewable with no account behind it and nothing of it ships.

Holding a turn offers what `TurnMenu.items(for:)` says it offers, which is a list of values rather than a block of buttons: a context menu is a gesture no test can press, so which items each role gets is read there and drawn in the row.

## The row at the end of the transcript

The person's next turn is written at the end of the transcript rather than in the glass: `Draft` and `DraftRow` in the same file, drawn on the phone alone since it is the only screen handed one. `Draft` is the whole of what the row shows — the words and whether the keyboard is up, both bound to the chat; whether the turn is on its way; what sends it; and what takes it back, nil where there is nothing to take back — and its `state` is one of `hidden`, `writing` and `inFlight`, so each of the row's states is a value a test can make.

The row is drawn at the size the turn it is about to become will be: an enclosure of `look.bubble`'s shape and `look.transcript.bodyFont`, with a field laid over a `Text` that is not drawn and is what sizes it, since a field asked for its own width takes every point on offer while a `Text` takes what the words need; so the row hugs its words, wraps rather than scrolling inside itself, and holds `look.draft.minimumWidth` when it is empty. What the row has of its own is `Look.Draft`: its two enclosures, that minimum width, the room beside the bubble, the slot the control sits in, and the control's type, ink and resting alpha.

The row is not a turn until it lands, and its colour says so: `look.draft.written` while it is being written, `look.draft.sending` while it is on its way, and the person's own `look.bubble` only in the log — three values of one type, each `Enclosure.bubble(screen)` with an accent of its own (`Theme.secondary`, `Theme.signal`), so the row keeps its shape as the turn lands and a look that changes one of the three leaves the other two alone. What else says the turn is on its way is the spinner in the send's slot and a field that cannot be typed into.

The row is shown whenever the keyboard is up or there is anything in it, so a caption from the microphone appears with no keyboard, and the transcript scrolls to it as it would to a turn; and it stays until its field has let the keyboard go (`Draft.holdsKeyboard`), however it was asked to go, since a field taken out of the window while it holds the keyboard drops the keyboard with no animation at all. The keyboard is the row's: the flank sets `typing`, the field takes focus from it, and the keyboard going down by any route clears it again.

## The badge, the jewel and the settings sheet

The chat's chrome is one mark: `Apps/Client/TopoBadge.swift`, a cabochon filling the control at the navigation bar's trailing edge with the octopus cut into it, where the title said "Topo" twice over. A tap opens the settings sheet and a hold opens the diagnostics, which the chat also presents itself so a screen that will not draw is still reachable. It has one state, because nothing on the phone measures standing: `RoleSelector` holds the role decided at launch and `PrimaryReader` reads a lease's freshness, and neither says whether the mind can be reached.

`StainedGlass`, the cabochon it is cut from, is handed a `Look.Jewel` and a diameter and draws nothing of its own: the body is a slice of jelly-teal glass (`agate`, a 512px disc in the iOS asset catalogue, a circular window onto `Design/topo-jelly.png` cut by `Design/make-icons.swift`, which grounds the app icons on the same stone and cuts the mark into them through the same `Pressed` treatment — so the gem under the thumb and the icon on the home screen are one object), filled as an `ImagePaint` so the light cut into it stays an inner shadow, under a cast — a colour at an alpha in a blend mode — so a cabochon of another colour is three values and never a second picture. The badge's is the stone under `Theme.secondary` (`look.badge.jewel`), the microphone's the same stone with no cast on it. The diameter is handed over rather than read off a `GeometryReader`, because a paint is scaled to the shape it fills and a reader resolves on a second layout pass.

The mark is pressed into the stone rather than laid on it: one treatment, `Pressed`, handed `look.press` — the wall's width as a share of the jewel's diameter (6/512, what the exploration measured), the dark wall above a stroke, the light one below it, and the shade over the floor — with the floor of the cut filled from the same stone aligned to the same jewel. It reads only the alpha of the mark it is handed, so the octopus's drawing and `mic.fill` go through the one treatment, and it fades that mark to nothing rather than hiding it, since a hidden view leaves the accessibility tree and the microphone is the element both UI suites find.

`Apps/Client/SettingsView.swift` is the sheet: a `Form` of Mind (the model), Voice (Read replies aloud, Vocabulary), Memory (where the vault's folder lives, `MemoryView`), Diagnostics and About, and Sign out, destructive, and last, Tuning (`Apps/Client/Tuning.swift`, this device's hand on the look: where Topo sits — a picker of the three placements — and, while he is pinned, the pin; in a debug build a slider each for `mascot.clearance`, `transcript.replyTrailingInset`, `transcript.personLeadingInset`, `mascot.scale` and `mascot.roamSpeed`, labelled with the value worn, and `Show his field`, the overlay of why Topo stands where he stands (`docs/mascot.md`), a defaults key of this device's (`topo.debug.mascotField`) rather than part of the override; and a Reset. What they set, and what a drag on Topo sets, is a `look.json` of the same shape kept in this device's defaults (`topo.tuning`) and decoded onto the vault's look by `LookDocument` in `wearing`, in every build, so its ranges and per-field fallbacks are the document's and it outranks the vault's `look.json` — a release build reads only the placement and the pin from it, having no sliders, so slider values a debug install left are neither worn nor kept (`Tuning.knobsWorn`); a debug build launched with `TOPO_DEBUG_TUNING` sets it, or with it empty removes it, before anything reads it (`DebugRun.tuning`). The chat's debug report carries the clearance, the placement and the pin worn and what the override holds of the placement and the pin (`DebugRun.ChatReport`), which `TuningSliderTests` in `TopoUITests` reads with a slider moved, with a pin kept and after Reset, and with the glass chosen in the picker).

Signing out is `SignOut`, a value the chat builds from the four things it ends — the speaker, the harness, the memory, the login, in that order, the harness awaited so what the guest kept of the conversation is gone before the login is — and hands to the sheet, so which of them end, and when, is what `SignOutTests` reads rather than a block inside a button.

## The composer

The bar under the transcript is `Apps/Client/Composer.swift`: a floating pane of glass a share of the screen's width wide, sitting off the bottom edge, with the microphone set into a well cut through the middle of it and a control at each end.

The glass is what says the microphone is open, because the thumb that opened it covers the jewel: the pane takes Topo's colour, the jewel goes pale, a glow spills onto the transcript behind, and the two flanks go to nothing while keeping their space, so the pane never changes size under the hand. The mark is `mic.fill`, `waveform` while the microphone is open hands-free, cut into the stone through the same `Pressed` the badge's octopus goes through, with `look.composer.glyph.openCast` laid over the floor of the cut while the microphone is open — a mark cut into pale stone has no ink to flip. A press that would be refused drains the colour out of the jewel and fades it, the diagnostics `speech` row saying why. The jewel is `StainedGlass` again, handed `look.jewel` at rest and `look.composer.openJewel` — the same stone under a milky veil — open, so both states are values and neither is a branch in a view; every other value the pane, the etch, the well and the mark are drawn with is a field of `Look.Composer`.

`Composer.MicState` is the mapping: the chat hands over `canListen`, `listening`, `owner` and `handsFree` read off `VoiceInput`, and the state says which of idle, held, hands-free and dimmed they are, so what the glass draws is a value a test can make. It asks the owner before the manner of opening, so a session another surface holds leaves this glass alone however it was opened. The gesture is the chat's, `onLongPressGesture(minimumDuration: 0, maximumDistance: 60)` on the whole of the well, handing the press to `ChatView.micPressed`, which is the whole of the screen's part in a session; the three accessibility labels are `VoiceInput`'s state in words, and they are the only route `MicrophonePressTests`, `TopoOnTheGlassTests` and `SpokenTurnTests` have to the button.

The leading control raises the keyboard and lowers it again, which is what shows the row at the end of the transcript and gives it the keyboard; the pane itself holds no field, since the words belong in the bubble they are about to become. The keyboard control is on the leading flank; the trailing control keeps its space and draws nothing, since what else can come in besides words has no path into the log yet. The whole pane is off limits to a roaming Topo (`docs/mascot.md`), at every presence and at both heights; a Topo placed on the glass stands on it.

The pane's surface is drawn at a presence, 0 to 1 (`Composer.presence`): nothing over the empty end of the transcript, where glass over a flat background is a lens with an edge and nothing behind it, and the pane whole where turns run under it. The chat works it out from the transcript's scroll geometry (`PanePresence.of`, a pure function beside `MicState`), 0 while the content's bottom edge is above the pane's top and rising over `look.composer.presenceRise` points of content running under it, in `look.composer.presenceDuration`; an open microphone is 1 whatever the geometry, since the tinted pane is what says the microphone is open, and so is the keyboard on screen, since the pane goes short under it and a pane that is not there cannot be seen to, and so is Topo placed on the glass (`holdsTopo`), since a Topo on invisible glass is a Topo floating. For the presence the keyboard is the row's field holding focus, which `DraftRow` reports through `Draft.focused` to the chat, and not `NextTurn.typing`, which outlives the field while a turn is on its way; focus changes in a transaction of its own ahead of the keyboard, so the surface fades in where it stands and then rides up with the pane. The two edges are measured in one named space on the chat, the content's bottom from `onScrollGeometryChange` (iOS 18) and both top edges from `onGeometryChange`, so the offer card and the keyboard's rise move the pane's edge rather than being assumed away. What the scroll geometry gives is the content's y at the top of the scroll view's frame, which reaches under the navigation bar; the top inset comes off to put it at the transcript's own edge, and the stack's trailing padding comes off with it, since those points are content that draws nothing and a pane over them is a pane over nothing. On iOS 17, the deployment target, there is no scroll geometry and the presence is 1, one of the screen's two availability branches (the other is Topo's reading of a reply's lines, `docs/mascot.md`).

The bar itself is `safeAreaInset(edge: .bottom)` on every OS: the transcript scrolls under the pane while there is more to scroll and at rest the last turn stops above it, and what says whether anything is behind the pane is the presence and not the modifier. `safeAreaBar` is not used — on a device it draws a bar background of its own behind the lozenge, a second slab wider than the pane.

Under the keyboard the pane is short: `ComposerGeometry.of` (a pure function beside `PanePresence`) draws the well, the jewel and the mark cut into it, and the room above and below the well, at `look.composer.compactShare` of their resting size (⅔, read from 0.5 to 1), so the pane is that share of its resting height wherever the well sets it; the flanks keep their size and are centred on the short row, and the well keeps its resting width in the row (`ComposerGeometry.slot`), so the flanks do not move and the pane's width and foot do not either, on a look whose content is wider than its share of the screen as much as any other. The well is never drawn under `Look.Composer.Well.pressable` (44 points, not a field, since it is the floor a look may not go under) for the keyboard, and the jewel is never drawn bigger than its well at either height. The microphone is drawn at its resting size and scaled as one piece, with the gesture and a `contentShape(Circle())` on the frame as drawn, so a press lands on the short well. The pane's corner radius goes short with it (`ComposerGeometry.cornerRadius`, the resting radius at the same share), since the short pane is the resting one scaled.

Whether the pane is short is the keyboard's own safe area and not the focus: the chat's body is a `GeometryReader` over the whole screen, and the bottom inset past the screen's own (`KeyboardInset.isUp`, against the same inset read with the keyboard's region ignored) is the keyboard on screen. That is read in the transaction the keyboard's rise and fall is laid out in, and nothing in the composer animates it on a time of its own, so the pane goes short and tall again on the keyboard's own curve, reaching its end with the keyboard, and the transcript is laid out once for both.

The composer publishes the pane's bounds and the well's as anchors (`ComposerFrames`, and again into `MascotScene` for Topo), and `ComposerGeometryTests` holds, as arithmetic at every end of the ranges of the share, the well, the jewel, the pane's width and both insets together, the short well at or over the floor, the jewel inside it, the short pane the share of the resting one and never taller, and its corner radius the resting one at the same share; and, reading those anchors in a 320-by-568 window at every look among them whose resting pane fits that screen, the short well at the geometry's size, inside the short pane top to foot and side to side wherever the resting well is, and the pane exactly as wide and in the same place; `TopoOnTheGlassTests` raises the keyboard with the flank, in a launch with `TOPO_DEBUG_SOFTWARE_KEYBOARD` (`DebugRun.softwareKeyboard`), since a simulator starts with the Mac's keyboard connected and under it no keyboard rises, holds the microphone at two thirds of its size, and presses the short well at its edge.
