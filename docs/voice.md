# Voice

Push to talk, the on-device ear and voice, the audio session and the background hold, and how the models get onto the phone. The invariants a change here must keep are in CLAUDE.md's *Where the risk lives* (audio lifecycle, the press path).

## Push to talk, the ear and the voice

Voice on the phone is push to talk (`Apps/Client/VoiceInput.swift`): hold the microphone to talk and release to send, or tap to open it until the next press; the first-run answer is hold-to-talk only. The first press asks for the microphone, which is the only permission a press asks for, and nothing is asked before it.

Recognition is on the device, and it is the phone's one ear: Parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2 through FluidAudio (`Apps/Client/Ear.swift`, the iOS target only), the audio so far decoded bare every second for the caption and the whole utterance decoded and rescored at the release against the person's own vocabulary (`Apps/Client/Vocabulary.swift`: empty until they add to it, since none of anyone else's words are theirs; edited from the settings sheet; kept in this device's defaults rather than the vault, because a vocabulary is this device's ear and not the person's memory). An edit rebuilds the spotter's session live, so a word works from the next press; the gate is constant (no aliases, similarity 0.65, three characters minimum: Daphne's measured config, and the reason additions are safe). The session is a layer over Parakeet, not a condition of the ear: an empty list builds none, and one that cannot be built leaves Parakeet recognising bare, with the reason in the diagnostics `speech` row.

The models are not in the bundle: about 540 MB of CoreML that `ModelDownloads` (`Apps/Client/ModelDownloads.swift`) fetches from the first foreground on (`VoiceInput.prepare`) into the app's Application Support, where FluidAudio loads them from, told to touch no network of its own (`ModelHub.offlineMode`). Until they are resident, or if they fail to load, a press is refused with the ear's own status line and nothing touches the audio session or the engine; both surfaces' microphone buttons, the chat's and the first run's, are dimmed whenever a press would not open one (`VoiceInput.canListen`, the microphone denied or the ear not resident, read from the state as it is, so the load finishing undims them with no press in between); the first run's copy about allowing it in Settings stays tied to `denied` alone, since an ear that is still downloading is a wait rather than something to go and allow; a decode that throws at the release sends the caption the glances left, and nothing when there is none, with the error in the debug `Capture`. The diagnostics screen's `speech` row says which state the ear is in.

A spoken turn's reply is read aloud (`Speaker`; a setting in the settings sheet), a typed one's is not; holding any of Topo's turns in the transcript opens a menu that says it again, which is how a typed turn's reply is heard (`Replay` in `Apps/Client/TranscriptView.swift`, threaded from the chat screen, so the watch, the television and a viewer offer nothing). The item reads Stop while the speaker is speaking, and is gone while the voice is not resident.

The voice is on the device, and it is the phone's one voice: Kyutai's Pocket TTS through FluidAudio (`Apps/Client/Voice.swift`, the iOS target only), speaking its stock `eponine`: each person names their own mind, so the default voice is a neutral stock one rather than anyone's blend. Every stage is placed off the GPU — the conditioner, the flow-LM and the fused flow decoder at `.cpuAndNeuralEngine` on the rank-4 `.ane` variants, Mimi at `.cpuOnly`, where the port found it fastest and where its fp16 state feedback does not beep; `.all` is never used, because it lets a stage fall to the GPU. A reply is cut into sentences on sentence-final punctuation, each synthesised behind the one before as a stream of 80 ms frames pushed to one player node as they decode, so the wait is the first frame. Pocket has no pace of its own, so the pacing is the play queue's `AVAudioUnitTimePitch` at `Voice.tempo`, 1.20 with the pitch untouched: a per-speaker constant, explained in the doc comment, and the only thing between the voice's frames and the speaker. `Speaker` reads the clock through an injected `now` (the continuous clock in production), so the debug report's two measurements are arithmetic a unit test drives rather than a race it has to wait out. The model is not in the bundle either: about 351 MB (the four CoreML bundles the ANE placement loads, the constants and the tokenizer beside them, and the one speaker) that `ModelDownloads` fetches on the same cue (`Speaker.prepare`) and FluidAudio loads from the store, told to touch no network of its own. A reply is not read aloud at all when the model is not resident or fails to load — there is nothing to fall back to — and the diagnostics screen's `voice` row says which state the model is in; a debug build launched with `TOPO_DEBUG_VOICE=loading` (`DebugRun.voice`) holds the voice unloaded, and one launched with a directory loads Pocket from there instead of downloading it, which is what the talk lane does.

One `VoiceInput` for the app and one gate on its microphone, enforced: a surface pressing while another holds it is refused, since two gates on one input answer the same utterance twice; the two surfaces are never mounted together and each cancels its session on disappearing. Every press is a session with a generation, so a release during the permission prompts starts nothing, a late recogniser result or a second release belongs to no session, and what one session heard cannot become the next one's turn. The audio session, the idle timer and the background hold are process-wide, so `AudioSession` counts claims on them rather than assigning them; the record configuration is warmed on the foreground so the press does not pay for the route change.

A spoken turn's reply is heard behind the lock, in a pocket, or in front of another app: the iOS target holds the `audio` background mode (`Apps/Topo/Info.plist`), and under it iOS runs the process only while an engine of its own is running, so the play queue keeps a second player node at no volume looping a fifth of a second of zeroes (the keeper) for as long as a hold stands. The process is held by the running engine and the keeper is only what it renders, so with no hold nothing runs: the last hold going stops the keeper and the engine with it, its nodes left attached, and the next hold or frame starts it again through the same refusal path as any other. An engine left running under a stopped keeper is a phone that never suspends. The hold is counted like the other two, with two kinds of owner: `.awaitingReply(nonce)`, one claim per spoken turn outstanding — the chat lets a second press append while the first turn is in flight, and each turn holds for itself — taken at the release and dropped when that turn's reply is handed to `speak`, when the harness says that turn failed (`Harness.onTurnFailed`, named as the turn ends, since the screen's error line cannot say whose turn stopped), when a press, a Stop, a sign-out or a reset ends things, and at its own ceiling of 120 s; and `.speaking`, taken in `speak` after `ensureActive` and dropped when every sentence is made **and** the queue is drained, since a queue that empties between two sentences is not the reply's end. Nothing is held for a turn whose reply could not be heard anyway — Read replies aloud off, or the voice not resident at the release — and a typed turn asks for none, so the app suspends behind the lock as it always did. Both owners are bounded by the same 120 s ceiling, `.awaitingReply` from the release and `.speaking` from the last frame scheduled or played back, so a hold outlives a dead reply by the ceiling at most; the ceiling's line says which way the wait ended, a reply that never landed or one that landed and was refused, with the count, because the two end the same wait and only that tells a run's log them apart.

An `AVAudioSession` interruption is not the end of either: iOS posts a `.began` at the lock screen with no call and no Siri in it, so `.began` stops the player, keeps the buffers nobody has heard and leaves the hold standing — and a frame arriving while the engine is dead is added to what is owed rather than played, since restarting a dead engine throws and the voice reads that throw as the sentence's end (the notification's type, reason, `wasSuspended` and options are logged), and `.ended` — or the `AVAudioEngineConfigurationChange` a real interruption's end posts — rebuilds through the same path as the lock's category flip, whenever anything is owed or the queue is dead, building an engine from nothing if an earlier refusal left none. Every way the recovery path can fail ends in one refusal: the engine is dropped, the owed frames and the dead mark are kept, the session is invalidated, and the next end, change or reply tries the whole thing again — an activation refused, an engine that will not start, a frame that could not be played, a keeper that could not be started. A reply arriving on a dead queue rebuilds it first and is refused when that rebuild is, and one whose hold cannot be honoured — the keeper's own engine refusing to start — is refused the same way, since a reply taken on an engine that will not start would clear its turn's mark and end its wait for a reading that never happens. The hold's count tells the speaker about every claim taken or dropped, not only about the answer changing, so a claim arriving while another already stands is the chance to start a keeper an earlier refusal left down. Nothing is reported as playing, or as held, that is not. The format survives a refusal, because it is the voice's rate and not the engine's and a frame arriving while there is no engine at all is still made against it and owed; only a media services reset drops it. A reply cut by a short call carries on after it; a reply the speaker refuses (`speak` answers whether it took it) is still owed, so its turn stays marked spoken, its wait stands to its ceiling, and the next pass offers the reply again.

The decision to read a reply aloud is `Harness.onReply`, which the chat installs beside `PushWake`: it fires for a reply this phone wrote and for one a pass read that another primary wrote, once per turn, because behind the lock nothing is drawn and whether a view's observer runs is SwiftUI's to decide. A reply is recorded as offered only when a handler took it — one that was not there, and one that could not act, both leave it to be offered again — and installing a handler replays what arrived unheard, since the screen reads the log and sends what an earlier launch left owed before it installs. Of more than one owed reply the newest is read and the older are cleared unread: a person coming back is owed the answer to the last thing they said rather than a backlog read at them, and each reply spoken cuts off the one before it anyway. The handler reads the mark and not the setting, since the setting governs what the next release decides and not what a turn already released is owed. Whether a turn is spoken is the wait's own answer — `Speaker.awaitReply` answers both `spoken` (the reply will be read aloud when it lands, which is what marks the turn, and is true in the foreground whatever the audio is doing) and `held` (the keeper is actually rendering for it); the chat records the mark from `spoken` and states no condition of its own, so no later launch turning the setting on reads an old reply aloud, and a keeper that would not start is reported as the refusal it is rather than as a hold. The mark is the harness's, and on disk (`topo.harness.spoken`, the twenty most recent nonces owed a reply, cleared per turn as its reply is read and by a sign-out): the words said before the app went away survive the relaunch in the outbox, so what makes them a spoken turn has to survive with them. Signing out, and the far end of a takeover, stop the speaker: the reply in the ear goes with the login.

While any hold stands the session is not reconfigured: a claim change — the warm record claim dropped at the lock, or any other — is remembered, so the session that was active at the release stays active and untouched, whatever category it was in, until the reply has been heard. What is remembered is applied when the last hold goes and the app is in front; behind the lock, where the last hold going is as likely a reply ending as the app coming back, the session is marked as needing configuring instead and the claims are left for the next path that wants audio — the next `ensureActive`, or the foreground's own warm claim, which applies them as it changes. Activating or reconfiguring a non-mixable session from the background is refused (`cannotInterruptOthers`), and a session left invalid by that refusal is one every later `ensureActive` tries the same refused call on, which is a reply spoken only on unlock; an already-active session goes on rendering behind the lock under the `audio` mode, so `ensureActive` at `speak` is free there and nothing sets a category or activates a session from the background. A claim's configure failing is logged with its error rather than thrown, since a claim is not an audio path and the path that is meets it at `ensureActive`. What can still change under a hold is an interruption, whose end is exactly when iOS allows the session back: the queue rebuilds for its own engine on an `.ended` or an `AVAudioEngineConfigurationChange` — `ensureActive` first, then a fresh engine at the same format, every buffer not yet played back scheduled again in order under a new epoch so the discarded completions move nothing, and the keeper back if a hold stands. Resuming a reply after an interruption ends is not built.

Nothing that is a handle into mediaserverd — an `AVAudioEngine`, its input node, a tap, an `AVAudioPlayerNode` — is created, read, started or spoken to until `AudioSession` has configured **and** activated the session since the last reset; a failure to activate is a refusal, never a fall-through. `AudioSession.ensureActive()` is the call every audio path makes first, and costs nothing on a session that is already valid, so a press still does not pay for the route change the foreground paid for.

When iOS resets the media server (`AVAudioSession.mediaServicesWereResetNotification`) the session is marked invalid and re-applied if it had ever been configured, a failure there being remembered rather than swallowed; `VoiceInput` cancels any press, dropping its words as an interruption does, and drops its engine; and `Speaker` stops the reply, drops both holds and drops the play queue's engine, players and time-pitch unit, which an interruption deliberately does not. Nothing is rebuilt in a handler: the press and `speak` each reactivate and build what they need, so recovery does not depend on which of the two comes first, and Say it again after a reset works with no press before it. A press reads the input node's client and hardware formats and refuses (`no audio input`) when either has no sample rate or no channel or the two rates differ — both are ways `installTap` raises an exception no `catch` sees — and a refusal, like an activation that threw, drops the engine and invalidates the session so the next press rebuilds rather than reading the same dead node. Lifted from Daphne, per `THIRD-PARTY`.

## Model downloads

On-device model weights are downloaded by Topo, never by the libraries: one background `URLSession` (`ModelDownloads`, identifier `zone.hexagon.topo.models`; expensive and constrained network access off, waits for connectivity, not discretionary) so iOS applies the person's data settings and finishes the transfer with the app suspended or gone, with no prompt, toggle or gate of ours. What it fetches is `Apps/Topo/Resources/models.json`, the pinned manifest `scripts/model-manifest.sh` writes from the Hugging Face tree API: `FluidInference/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2-coreml` at `ee09c569`, `FluidInference/parakeet-ctc-110m-coreml` at `accdafd8`, `FluidInference/pocket-tts-coreml` at `91748676` (the English v2.1 pack, and of it only what the ANE placement loads); each entry is the flattened file list (a `.mlmodelc` is a directory of files on the Hub) with sizes and sha256 digests. Download tasks only, one per file, each verified against its digest and moved into `Application Support/Models/<id>/` with the digest recorded in the directory's ledger (two entries live elsewhere, `ModelStore.homes`, because the library that reads them reads one path and no other: the CTC spotter's is FluidAudio's own `CtcModels.defaultCacheDirectory`, where `VocabularyBoostingSession` reads its tokenizer, and the voice's pack is `Models/pocket-tts/`, where `PocketTtsManager` looks beneath the base directory it is given, with the manifest's paths kept repository-relative so each file lands where the loader looks; a set downloaded under the root before that is renamed into place rather than fetched again); a model is present only when every file is on disk at its size and in the ledger, a partial set carries on from the files it has (resume data where the server gave any), and a bump is a new revision in the script and a re-run.

`TopoAppDelegate` (a `UIApplicationDelegateAdaptor`) takes `handleEventsForBackgroundURLSession`, so a transfer that finished while the app was dead is admitted on the relaunch and the handler called. The ear's and the voice's status lines, and the diagnostics `speech` and `voice` rows, say what the session reports: waiting for a network the settings allow, downloading with bytes done of total, verifying, then preparing the model for this phone (the CoreML load, which after an install is the Neural Engine compiling the models afresh) and resident.

The store keeps only what the manifest accounts for (`ModelStore.sweep`, `Apps/Client/ModelHousekeeping.swift`, a function over the manifest rather than a step of the download path), run at launch for every model already present and again after each arrival that makes one present. The protected set is each entry's directory as `directory(for:)` resolves it, never its id, since the voice's id is `pocket-tts-coreml` and its home `Models/pocket-tts`: anything under `Models/` that is none of those homes is removed, and inside a home under `Models/` whose model is present, anything that is not a manifest file, the ledger or a file's resume data. A model that is not present is not swept at all, and a home outside `Models/` (the spotter's, FluidAudio's own cache) is a library's and is never entered. Every folder from Application Support down to what the sweep removes or enters, `Models/` included, is judged with `lstat`: a `Models/` reached through a link is not swept at all, a home reached through one is not entered, a link inside a home is removed as a link and never descended, and the link a skipped `Models/` or home is reached through is left where it stands.

CoreML's compiled Neural Engine bundles land in `Library/Caches/<bundle id>/com.apple.e5rt.e5bundlecache`, a generation per install that nothing takes away, so that directory, and nothing else in Caches, is removed whole on the first launch of a new install (`CompileCache`; not at all, and nothing recorded, when Caches or the app's folder in it is reached through a link), before the ear and the voice are made (`ModelHousekeeping.launch` in `TopoApp.init`, since a debug build's start loading as they are made). An install is the `LC_UUID` of the image the app's code is linked into (the main executable in a release build, `Topo.debug.dylib` in a debug one) together with the app bundle's path, whose container directory every install makes new, so a byte-identical build installed over itself is a new install too; it is compared with the one last recorded in defaults (`topo.models.compileCacheInstall`), because `CFBundleVersion` is 1 on every development install, and a bundle path that moved on a plain launch would cost one recompile and no file; the key is recorded only once the directory is gone or was never there, so a removal that fails is tried again on the next launch.
