# Centralisation audit, November 2026

A read of the whole tree at `b249e1c` (444 Swift files, 128K lines, with `scripts/`, the workflows and `Womble/`) for things that several engineers, each given one feature, built independently and that should be one shared thing: the shape the sign-out teardown had before it was gathered up, in whatever form it takes elsewhere. Every claim cites `path:line` at that commit, and where two bodies are called identical, `diff` was run on them. Womble is its own bundle by design (iOS 15, no TopoCore), so its copies of client code are noted and not ranked; TopoMascot is left out because it is copied unchanged from samdu/experiments. Line counts are the lines a shared thing would let go of, estimated from the bodies cited, not a promise.

## Verdict

The pattern the sign-out had is the house style: it recurs in at least twenty more places, in the app, in the packages, in the tests and in the scripts. The sign-out itself is not finished. No type, doc, commit, branch or open PR at `b249e1c` names a `LoginLife`; what exists is three hand-wired closure structs (`SignOut`, `Takeover`, `ViewerArrival`) beside three other routes by which a login's end reaches its listeners, and one of those routes runs every registered hook twice per sign-out. The worst of the rest, in order: the sequenced-record family (the log, the board and the vault read by three copies of one algorithm, with the invariant's reading side owned by nobody), the loopback listener lifecycle (about 150 lines in the proxy and the tool service identical line for line but one comment, and a second pair in TopoLink), the thirty-five generation counters compared by hand twelve ways, and, on the test side, thirty-nine `RecordDatabase` test wrappers that forward four methods by hand, thirty-eight of which leave the lease's request bound to a protocol default that cannot reach the ten that stall on purpose. About 3,000 lines could go. The lines matter less than the shape: every row below is a place the next feature's engineer will write a private copy unless a hook is named for them to use, which the two proposals at the end are for.

## 1. A login's end and the role's change reach their listeners by four routes

This is the sign-out the request names, as it stands at `b249e1c`. Three closure structs hold the teardown: `SignOut` with seven steps (`Apps/Client/SettingsView.swift:149-182`), `Takeover` with eight (`:188-216`) and `ViewerArrival` with seven (`:223-243`), the same closures in three orders, each built by hand, twice in `Apps/Client/ChatView.swift:474-490` and once in `Apps/Client/RootView.swift:29-37`. No step has a default, which the comment at `:187` says is so a caller that leaves one out does not compile; it does not stop a feature from never being a step at all.

Three more routes exist beside the structs. `SurfaceReloader.onForget` (`Apps/Topo/SurfaceReloader.swift:26-32`) is a subscriber list that `forget` runs (`:84-88`), registered from `Apps/Topo/SurfaceSync.swift:103`, `Apps/Topo/WidgetTaps.swift:315`, `Apps/Topo/ActivityKeeper.swift:248` and `Apps/Topo/TopoApp.swift:127`, with `TopoApp.swift:131-134` constructing two singletons early only so that their hooks are registered. The login's phase has four followers under `.onChange(of: signIn.phase)` (`TopoApp.swift:265-275`): `MemoryWake.follow`, `DefaultSurface.follow` (`Apps/Topo/DefaultSurface.swift:47-53`), `ControlDefaults.follow` (`Apps/Topo/ControlDefaults.swift:25`) and `ShareInbox.follow` (`Apps/Topo/ShareInbox.swift:69`), and each decodes the transition itself in its own spelling: `was == .signedIn, phase != .signedIn`; `phase == .signedIn ... replacing: was != .signedIn`; an `if`/`else if` over both. The role's change has a fourth bus, `.onChange(of: roleSelector.role == .primary && signIn.phase == .signedIn)` (`TopoApp.swift:277-284`), with `SurfaceSync.sweep`, `ScreenTool.follow` (`Apps/Topo/Tools/ScreenTool.swift:49`, into `ScreenStore.follow` at `Apps/Shared/ScreenStore.swift:106`) and `shares.follow` a second time.

The routes cross. `SignOut.act` runs `forgetSurfaces`, which calls `SurfaceReloader.forget`, which runs every `onForget` hook and reloads every timeline; then `forgetLogin` calls `signIn.signOut`, the phase leaves `.signedIn`, and `DefaultSurface.follow` calls `reloader.forget(store())` again (`DefaultSurface.swift:49`). Every registered hook runs twice per sign-out, `SurfaceSync.forget` counts two sign-outs for one, and every timeline is reloaded twice. Nothing is broken by it today because each hook is idempotent; a hook that is not, such as one that mints a nonce or writes a tombstone under a tag, would be.

Below the buses, each owner keeps its own idea of "the login ended". `Harness.forget` (`Apps/Client/Harness.swift:437-439`) removes the chat view's `firstRunAnswered` key from `UserDefaults.standard` by literal. The harness bumps three counters at three moments of one sign-out (`login` at `:453`, `logins` at `:431`, `giving` at `:517`) and raises a fourth, `leaving` (`:441`), as a gate for as long as the guest's forget takes. Two stores keep a login "door" on disk, each with its own UUID and its own read-before-and-after check: `ShareStore.Door` (`Apps/Shared/ShareStore.swift:139-191`, with a lock) and `ScreenStore.Door` (`Apps/Shared/ScreenStore.swift:50-108`, without). Three steps that can fail keep their own owed mark in defaults, each with its own retry cue and its own report: `SignIn.signOutOwedKey` (a logout owed, retried at launch), `ConnectionsLeftBehind` (a keychain clear refused, retried at the next sign-in and reported as words), `SurfaceSync.forgetKey` (records' clear owed, retried at `flush` and reported nowhere).

The central thing is needed and does not exist. The user's brief names a `LoginLife` hook that replaced six teardowns this week; at `b249e1c` no type, doc, commit message, remote branch or open PR carries that name, and `docs/auth.md:16` still describes the sign-out as "each ends with `SignIn.signOut`, last, after the harness, the memory and the connections". If the hook is on a branch not yet pushed, this row is where it lands; if the three structs are what the brief means, they are a third of the job. One registration point with an ordered, named list of steps (shares, harness, memory, surfaces, connections, login) that `SignOut`, `Takeover` and `ViewerArrival` are three parameterisations of, that `SurfaceReloader.forget` is called from exactly once, that the phase followers subscribe to instead of decoding the phase, and that owns the owed marks, is the shared thing. The tests reach the same conclusion from the other side: `Tests/Client/SignOutTests.swift` reads the struct's order, and nothing tests the four buses together, which is why the double fire is not caught.

## 2. The sequenced-record family: one read algorithm, three copies

The log, the board and the vault are three families of per-device sequenced records with a shared writer and three readers. The write side is shared by `SequenceAppender` and `RecordNaming` and is the counter-example to cite. The read side is the same algorithm three times: `TurnLog.read` (`Packages/TopoCore/Sources/TopoCore/TurnLog.swift:258-272`), `BoardStore.read` (`BoardStore.swift:26-39`) and `MemoryStore.read` (`MemoryStore.swift:33-50`) each fetch the type, classify, probe every device's next sequence by id and union what the query index hid into the missing set; `read(device:after:)` at `TurnLog.swift:290-296`, `BoardStore.swift:42-48`, `MemoryStore.swift:54-60`; `writer(for:)` at `:301-306`, `:50-55`, `:65-70`; the `X(from records:)` classifier at `:308-322`, `:57-71`, `:99-113`.

The value types repeat with them. `TurnRef` (`TurnLog.swift:14-35`), `CardRef` (`Board.swift:25-45`) and `NoteRef` (`MemoryVault.swift:63-84`) are the same struct with the same `<` and the same `init?(parsing:)` over the last slash; `ref(ofRecordNamed:)` is at `TurnLog.swift:86-89`, `Board.swift:93-96`, `MemoryVault.swift:128-131`; the parents parse-all-or-nil at `:118-120`, `:121-123`, `:159-161`; the missing/lastSeen/heads computation in `Transcript.init` (`TurnLog.swift:155-174`), `Board.init` (`Board.swift:164-178`) and `Vault.init` (`MemoryVault.swift:210-224`); the `SequenceError` to domain error mapping at `TurnLog.swift:393-400`, `BoardStore.swift:159-163`, `MemoryStore.swift:189-193`; the sync wrapper at `:453-459`, `:166-172`, `:198-204`; and three error enums each with `sequenceContended`, a `markerWithout…` and an `incomplete…` case.

They have already diverged. Only `TurnLog.read` computes `free` (`TurnLog.swift:153,270`); the other two do not know the concept. `Transcript.ordered` drops a turn whose parents form a cycle, where Womble's copy appends it, and no TopoCore test covers a cycle. The compare-and-set retry loop around a single record is a fourth copy of a related shape, written in `DeviceDirectory.register` (`Pairing.swift:146-165`, three tries), `pair` (`:187-208`, five), `update` (`:211-224`, five) and `MemoryStore.setRemote` (`MemoryStore.swift:83-97`, four), each with its own `contended` error. A generic `SequencedLog<Ref, Revision>` carrying the read, the ref, the classifier, the heads and the error map, with `TurnLog`, `BoardStore` and `MemoryStore` as three instantiations, is the shared thing; the append-only invariant CLAUDE.md names would then have one reading side to review.

## 3. The loopback listener lifecycle, twice verbatim and twice again

`APIProxy` (`Packages/TopoProxy/Sources/TopoProxy/APIProxy.swift:46-229`) and `ToolService` (`Packages/TopoProxy/Sources/TopoTools/ToolService.swift:45-238`) carry the same listener lifecycle, about 150 lines each, identical line for line but one comment: the state (`listener`, `generation`, `queue`, `waiting`, `connections`, `port`), `start`, `relisten(on:)` with the 25 × 20 ms `EADDRINUSE` loop (`:73-89` ≡ `:85-101`), `listen(requesting:)` (`:91-115`, `:103-126`, the one comment apart), `relisten()`, `stop`, `stateChanged` (`:143-174` ≡ `:151-182`), `lost` (`:177-181` ≡ `:185-189`), the four test seams, `down`, `settle`, `accept` and `forget`. The only difference is the `Inbound` slack (+64K against +1K) and what `accept` dispatches to. Both `lost` bodies log the string `"listener lost ("`, which `Apps/Topo/GuestResident.swift:249` matches by prefix: a log line is the contract between three types, and the prefix is spelled in three places.

TopoLink has the second pair. `LeaseProbeServer.start`/`stateChanged` (`Packages/TopoLink/Sources/TopoLink/LeaseProbeServer.swift:62-99`) and `SurfacePageServer.start`/`stateChanged` (`SurfacePageServer.swift:51-86`) are the same body, differing in the queue's label and in how the `ready` continuation is spelled; `receiveSome` (`LeaseProbeServer.swift:201-209`) and `receiveChunk` (`SurfacePageServer.swift:235-243`) are the same shim; `SocketLeaseProbe.confirms` (`LeaseProbeServer.swift:131-138`) and `SurfaceBrowser.ask` (`Surfaces.swift:198-205`) carry the same deadline and the same comment; `LANPresence` and `SurfaceBrowser` share one `stateUpdateHandler` word for word (`LANPresence.swift:28-36` ≡ `Surfaces.swift:139-147`) and the same observer list over a different element type (`LANPresence.swift:50-62`, `Surfaces.swift:157-168`), so TopoLink opens two `NWBrowser`s on the one `_topo._tcp` type with the same handlers (`LANPresence.swift:18-39`, `Surfaces.swift:127-150`). `DNSForwarder` (`Packages/TopoProxy/Sources/TopoProxy/DNSForwarder.swift:119-149,165-203,220-246`) is a third shape of the same thing with a serial-queue executor, a `Waiting` box and five attempts. Womble's `SurfaceServer` is a sixth, by rule.

The pairs have diverged where it matters. The proxy pair keeps `waiting` as a list of continuations and settles them all; the TopoLink pair keeps a single `ready` slot unguarded, so two `start()` calls before the listener is ready leave one continuation never resumed.
