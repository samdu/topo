# The phone harness

How a turn is taken on the phone: the runner, the one seam to a model, the row at the end of the transcript that holds a turn until it lands, and the push that wakes the answering loop. The brain the app composes is the guest, `docs/guest.md`; the log and lease underneath are `docs/cloudkit.md`.

## The runner and the brain

The phone harness is `Packages/TopoTurn`: `TurnRunner` (take the primary lease, append the person's turn under a nonce the caller keeps until the turn is known to be in the log, ask the `Brain`, append the reply as its child in one atomic batch with a lease heartbeat; a failed answer leaves the person's turn in the log, a lease another device took leaves the reply out, and one that only lapsed while the model answered is claimed afresh in the reply's own batch, `docs/cloudkit.md`) and `Brain` (`Sources/TopoTurn/Brain.swift`), the one seam between the runner and a model: a `BrainRequest` (the log as read, the person's turns being answered, the reply's parents and nonce, the model) in and a reply out, told when a reply is in the log (`landed`), asked which person turns it holds as unresolved and whether it owes the log a reply it finished before a crash. The runner writes an owed reply, under its own nonce and parents, before it writes or answers anything else, and `answerPending` does not answer an unresolved head; a pass that wrote only an owed reply returns it, so the harness draws it and takes down the failure its first save put up, as for a reply it asked for. The one brain is Claude Code in the guest (`GuestBridge`, `docs/guest.md`; `Harness.standard`), and there is no fallback from it; the package's own suite drives the runner over a scripted brain, and the app's suites drive the harness over `GuestBridge` with a scripted guest. A reply's nonce is a hash of the turns it answers, so a retry or a second brain finds the one reply.

The row at the end of the transcript is drawn against that contract: it holds the words under the nonce `willSend` gave them until `Harness.said(nonce)` is true, which is what the outbox's own retry is keyed to, so a turn that never settled stays in flight and one whose reply failed clears, its turn standing in the log. `Harness.withdraw(nonce)` is the way back from the first: it takes the entry off the line so the words can be said again as one turn under one nonce, and it asks the log before it does — a turn already there cannot be taken back, and neither can one whose log could not be read, since a read that failed is not an answer. It is refused while an attempt is running, which is a write that may be landing as it is asked. The row is `NextTurn` (`Apps/Client/NextTurn.swift`) rather than the chat screen's own state, because what it holds outlives the screen: the line is on disk, so a chat appearing over an unsettled line takes the oldest words on it whose turn the read did not find in the log back up as the turn on its way (`resume(from:)`), which is where the way back from a turn that never landed is and where it is most needed. The oldest still owed and not the head, since the head can be a turn that reached the log and lost its acknowledgement, with a turn said behind it still owed; words already in the log belong to the transcript and not to the row, whichever entry they are. It resumes nothing over a row already in use. It refuses a landed turn's words while a turn is on its way (`edit(_:in:)`), since the row draws the words being sent until they land or are taken back.

A reply is drawn as it is written, before it is in the log. Claude Code writes a message's text a piece at a time (`StreamEvent.writing`, with `writingBegan` at each message's start), the bridge relays it as it relays every event, and `Harness.writing` holds what the guest has written of the turn: the words of every message so far, built by `ReplyWords` (`Packages/TopoUserland`) — a message's text runs on, a message that says nothing (one that only thought or called a tool) adds nothing, and one message's words are parted from the next's by a blank line — so what was said before a tool call stays while the tool runs and after; `ChatView` draws it as one more row under the transcript's turns. The reply the bridge writes to the log is built by the same function from the whole text blocks (`docs/guest.md`), so what the row shows when the stream ends is the reply that lands, byte for byte. It is not a turn: it goes when the reply lands in the transcript, at sign-out, at a takeover, and when the turn fails or its reply's save is refused, since the log is what the transcript shows. The reply to a spoken turn is read as it is written too: the harness tells `onWriting` the reply so far under the spoken turn's nonce, each telling extending the last and none replacing it, and `Speaker.speak(writing:answering:)` queues each sentence once it has settled (`Speaker.settled`: up to the last line break or sentence end, not into a code fence that has not closed, not into a table until a line that is no row follows it, since a table is read as the count of its rows — a row being a line that opens with a bar, so a bar in a line of prose holds nothing back — and no further into a block than an image in it until a blank line ends the block, since an image is read after its block), so the voice starts at the first sentence. The landed reply is still handed to `speak`, which queues only what was not yet read; a reading stopped meanwhile is not begun again, and one whose turn fails reads out what is queued and stops. A message the guest begins after a tool call is more of the same text: the harness tells `onWriting` the same text the row draws and nothing else, so what a reader is handed only grows: a new message adds nothing until it has words, and the blank line that comes with its first ones ends the last sentence of the message before, which is read then, with the new message's sentences behind it, each once (`SpokenReplyTests`, `GuestBridgeTests`). Nothing begins while a microphone is open or the voice is not resident: that reply is read when it lands, whole.

A line that stopped on a failure goes again three ways: the person saying something new, the button under the transcript, and the answering loop. A pass of `Harness.answering(every:)` whose read of the log got through sends a stopped line from its head, under the nonces it already carries, before it answers the log; a pass that could not read makes no attempt and counts for nothing. While the attempts keep failing the loop backs off in time, not in passes, by `Harness.retryBackoff`: one interval after the first failure, then two, four and eight, and twelve (a minute at the chat's five seconds) from then on, each measured from the failed attempt in elapsed time (`PrimaryLease.continuousUptime`), so a push's `wake()` running a pass early never brings an attempt forward, and setting the phone's clock neither brings it forward nor holds it back. A line that empties starts the count again, and so does a sign-out. The failure a read put up (the log's own words, with no prefix, so it fits the two lines of the navigation bar) is about the last read, so the next read that gets through takes it down; `Harness.failure` carries its source, so a read that gets through takes down a read's failure and no other, whatever the words.

`answerPending` is the log's own path for a limb's words: the primary answers any person turn at the log's head, whoever wrote it, one reply continuing every head; the phone runs it every five seconds while the chat is open, which is also how a reply that failed is retried. A phone that is not primary writes the person's turn as a limb and the primary answers it there.

The model is a setting, kept and sent as its family's alias: `sonnet` by default, `opus` or `fable` from the model slider on the composer (`docs/chat.md`), which Claude Code resolves to the latest of the family it knows, so the model answering moves with the guest's Claude Code pin (`scripts/model-manifest.sh`: 2.1.285 resolves them to Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1) and a setting kept as a model id is read as its family (`ClaudeModel.init(setting:)`). What the app calls each is the look's (`look.mind`, the family's name by default), so the mind renames them with `topo look set mind.sonnet …`. A debug build is pinned to Haiku 4.5 (`ClaudeModel.pinned`, through `ClaudeModel.effective`, which the bridge and the resident's launch both apply, and `APIProxy.pinnedModel` at the wire, so every request obeys it, not only the picker), because every debug build here is signed into a real subscription and a stray turn spends it. The setting reaches the guest as `--model`: a change restarts the resident process at its next idle moment, never mid-turn (`GuestSession.use(model:)`).

The iOS client runs the harness over the shared CloudKit log. With no socket, its lease probe answers no to every holder. Sign-out clears the tokens, the guest's token among them, the screen and the first-run answer; the log stays in the person's iCloud.

## The push that wakes the loop

A limb's turn wakes the answering loop by a silent CloudKit push rather than waiting out the five seconds. `TurnPush` (`Apps/Topo/TurnPush.swift`) is a `CKQuerySubscription` on `Turn` creation in the `Topo` zone, content-available only, with the predicate `sequence > 0` (every turn; a match-all would need the record-name index the development schema never builds, and `sequence` is queryable because `TurnLog.read(device:after:)` filters on it), saved once per Apple ID under a fixed id; the chat registers it beside its answering loop and ignores a failure.

`TopoAppDelegate` (`Apps/Topo/TopoAppDelegate.swift`) registers for remote notifications and hands a push that is the subscription's to `PushWake.handler`, which the chat installs immediately around `Harness.answering(every:)` and removes when it returns, so a push after sign-out or a takeover reaches nothing. The handler calls `Harness.wake()`, which cuts the loop's pause short and returns once the next pass has run; it never runs a pass beside the loop's, so two passes never race to make a runner or call the model. The push carries nothing, so it needs no authentication, and the loop is the backstop for every push Apple drops.

The iOS target alone has the `remote-notification` background mode (`Apps/Topo/Info.plist`) and `aps-environment` (`Apps/TopoiOS.entitlements`); the watch and TV keep `Apps/Topo.entitlements`, iCloud only. No suite delivers a real push: `TurnPushTests` holds the subscription's shape and the refusal of a push that is not ours, and `HarnessIntegrationTests` holds the wake.
